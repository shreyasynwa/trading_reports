Overnight Developments

The three things that changed between Tuesday's close and this morning's bell.

1. THE 10-YEAR FINALLY BACKED OFF — ~4.967% AFTER A SIX-SESSION CLIMB The benchmark 10-year yield eased more than two basis points to ~4.967%, its first decline in six sessions, after Tuesday took it to 5.04% — the highest since July 2007 — before settling near 5.01%. Fed funds futures still price ~92.5% odds of a 25bp hike at 2:00 PM to 3.75-4.00%, the first increase since 2023 and Chair Warsh's first move. The relief is mechanical rather than fundamental: a six-day run into a fully priced decision leaves positioning one-sided, and the crude softening removed the marginal inflation bid. It did not survive the data cleanly — August retail sales at 8:30 printed +1.2% headline and +1.4% control against +0.8% and +0.4% expected. A yield backing off into a 92.5%-priced decision is positioning, not a view. The dots at 2:00 PM decide whether ~4.97% was the fade or the pause.
2. CRUDE RELENTED ON LOGISTICS — SAUDI SHIP-TO-SHIP CARGOES AND A "DAYS" REPAIR CALL WTI fell 1.8% to ~$103.90 and Brent 0.9% to ~$107.80, extending losses through the morning toward ~$105 on Brent. Saudi Arabia is offering additional crude loadings to Asian refiners via ship-to-ship transfers off Oman's Sohar port after the September 10 drone strikes shut the ~1,200-km East-West pipeline that routes up to 7 mb/d toward the Red Sea. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright called the outage "a brief and temporary interruption" that "will be measured in days." Macquarie put Hormuz flows of crude, condensate and products above 7.5 mb/d since fighting resumed August 30 — resilient, and weakening the link between strait headlines and actual barrels. UBS's Giovanni Staunovo: concerns the disruption could be larger "are easing." Offsetting: a Houthi drone toward Mecca was intercepted, and CBO put the war's cost above $38B through August 1 at $2-3B monthly. This is a workaround, not a repair. Energy names sold it hard — FANG -4.7%, APA -5.2% — which is the correct expression to fade if the pipeline timeline slips.
3. INTEL AND SK HYNIX IN US MEMORY TALKS — THE FIRST CONSTRUCTIVE AI HEADLINE IN A WEEK INTC rose 3.3% to ~$100.37 and SKHY 3.3% premarket on Reuters reports the two are discussing a deal to manufacture memory chips on US soil for the first time. One scenario has SK Hynix leasing part of Intel's long-planned Ohio facility; another is a venture with Intel and cloud companies locking in HBM supply against Samsung and Micron. Both sides stressed the talks are exploratory, and Seoul's objection to offshoring sensitive HBM or DRAM technology is the live hurdle. The broader read-through was immediate: ASML +2.9% as AI-spending slowdown fears eased, DELL +2.5% on AI server demand and pending S&P 100 inclusion. Separately, OpenAI is in early talks on a round valuing it above $1.2T ahead of an eventual IPO. Last week's AI-pacing scare has now been answered twice — no capex revision, and a new supply-chain investment headline. The de-rating case needs an actual number, and it still doesn't have one.
