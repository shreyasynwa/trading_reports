What Happened Today

What actually drove today's tape — and what it hands to Monday.

1. THE 10-YEAR WENT STRAIGHT BACK TO 5.00% AND THE FRONT END MADE NEW HIGHS Treasury yields rose across the curve and took back Thursday's entire stabilization. The 10-year settled at 4.995%, up 6.1bp on the day, up 2.1bp on the week and up 27.4bp in September alone. The 2-year rose 5bp to 4.741% — a new 52-week high, and up roughly 57bp in a little over a month. The 3-year added 7.8bp to 4.84%, also a 52-week high. The 5-year finished 4.863%, the 7-year 4.929%, the 20-year 5.376%, the 30-year 5.327% (down 2.7bp on the week). The 2s10s spread reached its narrowest since March 2025. August industrial production gave no help: output was unchanged against a +0.3% consensus, capacity utilization printed 76.3% versus 76.4% expected, and ex-autos output rose just 0.1% after +0.3% in July. This is the single most important thing that happened today and it happened in the risk-negative direction. A bear-flattener led by a 2-year at 52-week highs is the market pricing more Fed, not less — and the long end stalling under 5% while the front end runs is the shape that precedes a growth problem, not an easing cycle.
2. THE INDEX HELD ON TWO SECTORS AND ALMOST NO PARTICIPATION Technology (+0.87%) and Industrials (+0.47%) were the only two S&P sectors to finish higher. All nine others declined. At 11:00 AM only 119 S&P constituents were advancing; the Russell 2000 closed with 551 of 1,944 holdings green and fell 0.50% on the session and solidly on the week. Only about 31% of S&P names sit above their 50-day moving average and roughly 50% above the 200-day, with eight of eleven sectors lower over the past month. The weekly picture is worse than the daily: XLY has now fallen six consecutive weeks, XLP, XLI and XLRE five, XLB four, with transports and small caps down five straight. For the week the S&P lost 0.1%, the Dow 1.7%, while the Nasdaq gained 0.7%. A +0.17% index print carried by two sectors with 119 advancers is a mega-cap bid, not a market. This is the narrowest tape of the quarter arriving on the day the dealer inventory that was smoothing it expired.
3. THE CRYPTO COMPLEX RIPPED ON AN SEC EXEMPTION THE SENATE REFUSED TO LEGISLATE The SEC issued a five-year conditional “Innovation Exemption” letting eligible venues trade tokenized versions of US-listed stocks without full traditional exchange registration — two days after the Senate failed to advance the CLARITY Act. Strategy (MSTR) +13.1%, Coinbase (COIN) +11.6%, MARA +10.2%, BitMine +7.9%, Robinhood (HOOD) +7.9%, Galaxy Digital +7.6%. Bitcoin rebounded above $76,700 after mid-week weakness. Separately, Sandisk (SNDK) rose 4.9% on AI data-center storage demand ahead of its S&P 100 inclusion on September 21. The regulator delivered by fiat what the legislature would not deliver by statute. That is a real change in the operating environment for COIN and HOOD, but it is also exactly the kind of gain that gets handed back when a rulemaking is challenged — and it is a five-year conditional exemption, not a law.
4. TWO TANKERS STRUCK IN HORMUZ AND CRUDE STILL FELL A UK maritime monitor reported a tanker hit by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz early Friday, causing a fire since extinguished, hours after a separate vessel was attacked in the same waterway. Iran claimed it struck a ship attempting an “illegal crossing.” WTI still settled at $100.30, down $1.61 (-1.58%), and Brent at $103.87, down 95 cents (-0.91%) — a third consecutive decline on Saudi ship-to-ship loadings off Oman, a partial East-West pipeline restart and product builds in the US, Singapore and Europe. President Trump said he faces a “big decision” on whether to launch a major assault to “annihilate” the Iranian regime, ahead of a meeting with all six GCC leaders at the UN next Tuesday. US average diesel hit a record $6.29 per gallon, up 68% from $3.74 a year ago. A barrel that falls on the day two ships are hit in the world's most important chokepoint is a barrel pricing logistics and ignoring tail risk. That is the definition of short gamma in the energy complex going into a weekend and a Tuesday summit.
5. THE YEN ROUND-TRIPPED ON SUSPECTED INTERVENTION The yen initially weakened more than 1.2% toward 158 per dollar after the BoJ's 25bp hike to 1.25% landed on a 7-2 vote, with traders reading the two dissents as a ceiling. Later in the session the currency reversed hard, falling back below 157 in what traders described as likely intervention, and settled near 156.68. EUR/USD finished 1.1483. This is the morning thesis getting tested inside one session. The premarket note flagged a snap back through 155 as the level that re-arms the carry unwind; the move stopped short of it, but the mechanism — an authority defending the currency after a hike failed to — is now demonstrated rather than hypothetical. Track dollar-yen, not the Nikkei, and treat 155 as live.
6. POSITIONING KEPT DRAINING WHILE THE INDEX HELD US equity funds recorded net redemptions of $31.44 billion in the week to September 18 — a fourth consecutive week of outflows, roughly matching the $32 billion withdrawn the prior week, per LSEG Lipper data. The NAAIM Exposure Index, which tracks active manager equity exposure, fell from above 100 to below 75. Meanwhile SpaceX (SPCX) had its Nasdaq-100 weighting roughly doubled in Friday's closing cross, from about 1.28% to about 2.82%, drawing an estimated $15.5 billion (Morgan Stanley) to $22 billion of passive buying, with the new weights effective before Monday's open. This is the cleanest bullish input on the page and it is a positioning argument, not a fundamental one. Four weeks of outflows and active exposure cut by a quarter means the marginal seller is closer to done than the tape suggests — which is why a narrow index still managed to close green.

Tomorrow's Setup — Three Paths Into the Open

Levels that matter: today's close at $761.69 is the pivot. Above $767 = continuation regime; below $757 = reversal regime; chop in between = pin mode.

Bull path
The nine-day collapse was real, positioning is washed out, and SPY reclaims 765

The constructive path runs on vol and flows rather than on fundamentals. VIX9D collapsed to 12.27 as the ~$7T expiration rolled off — down 5.1 points from Wednesday — which per the framework's own test means near-dated hedges were retired, not rolled. VIX closed 14.81. Positioning supports it: four straight weeks of US equity fund outflows totaling over $63B across the last two, and NAAIM exposure cut from above 100 to below 75. Monday brings mechanical demand — SPCX's Nasdaq-100 weight doubling to ~2.82% with $15.5-22B of passive buying effective at the open, plus SNDK into the S&P 100. Confirmation: SPY holds 757 through Monday's first hour, reclaims 765 on a close with the 10Y back under 5.00% and — critically — XLF green and IWM off its lows. The qualifier: the three-month vol that did not fall is the market telling you this path has a two-week shelf life.

Base case
A 757-765 grind where mega-cap masks a deteriorating tape

Base case, and the direct extension of Friday. SPY settled 761.69 with the index +0.17% on two green sectors and 119 advancers; QQQ +0.63%, IWM -0.47%. Working parameters: SPY 757-765 around the 761.69 anchor, 10Y 4.95-5.05%, 2Y 4.70-4.80% with the flattening intact, Brent $100-107 on two-sided headline risk, dollar-yen 155-158. Expect index-flow distortion at Monday's open from the SPCX reweight, then a thin drift as the tape waits for the PMIs and Tuesday's UN meeting. Posture: the index number will keep looking better than the market. Trade the dispersion — long the cohort that is actually being bought, flat or defined-risk short the cyclical and small-cap complex — rather than the index direction.

Bear path
The 2-year keeps making highs, 757 fails, and there is no inventory underneath

The bear path is already in motion and needs nothing new. The 2-year settled at a 52-week high 4.741%, the 3-year at a 52-week high 4.84%, the 10-year back at 4.995%, and 2s10s at its narrowest since March 2025 — all on a day August industrial production missed with output unchanged against +0.3% expected. Under that, the tape is already broken beneath the surface: two green sectors, 119 advancers, 31% of SPX above its 50-day, XLY down six straight weeks, XLP/XLI/XLRE down five, the Dow -1.7% on the week. Add the Hormuz tail — two tankers hit Friday, a president weighing whether to “annihilate” the regime, a GCC summit Tuesday — and a barrel that has been falling on logistics that are themselves under attack. Through 757 the tape sees 753, then 750 and 745, and this time there is no expiring open interest to slow it down: the ~$7T that was pinning it settled Friday.
