Regime
The single most important fact this morning is that the fix did not hold. On Wednesday the Treasury did something genuinely unusual — it at least doubled the maximum size of its liquidity-support buybacks from $2B to $4B and pointed them squarely at the 10-to-20 and 20-to-30-year sectors that had seen a buyers' strike since late June. It worked mechanically: the 30y fell about 9bp to 5.19% from Monday's 19-year high near 5.34%, the 10y dropped to 4.65%, TLT rallied 1.7%, and equities snapped a three-day losing streak with SPY +0.21% to 769.06 — though QQQ actually fell 0.20% and the entire index gain was XLV +3.51% on a 177% MRNA move. Less than 24 hours later the relief is more than half gone: 30y 5.24%, 10y 4.70%. The catalyst was macro, and it was emphatic. August Philly Fed general activity came in at +47.4 against a +25.0 consensus and a +41.4 prior — a five-year high — with the employment index at 27.9 versus 10.0, the average workweek at 26.5 versus 14.0, the six-month outlook at 73.6 versus 34.4, six-month capex intentions at 48.2 versus 30.1 and six-month prices received at 59.8 versus 41.4. Initial claims printed 206K against 210K expected. A hot regional survey with accelerating hiring, rising forward capex and rising forward pricing power is precisely the case the three FOMC dissenters made in Wednesday's 2:00 PM minutes, which explicitly flagged the potential for higher rates if inflation does not ease. Wells Fargo's read on the buyback was the right one — short-term relief that does not change the outlook for long-term yields — and the tape agreed within a session. The second thread is the consumer finally cracking at the top: Walmart beat on earnings but posted US comparable sales of +2.6% against +3.8% expected, its slowest in six years, with average ticket +1.1% versus +3.1% a year ago, drug-price declines from Medicare regulations weighing on US sales, and 750bps of adjusted operating income sourced from tariff refunds. The stock is down 8.6%. Against that, two things are genuinely constructive and worth respecting: semis have stopped going down — SMH +0.2% this morning with MU and WDC green and MRVL up 10% on a $12.2B Google custom-silicon deal — and the debasement trade is ripping, with BTC through $70K to $71,845, gold $4,543, silver $67.43 and EUR/USD at three-month highs.
Key binary
Is the long end's reversal a one-day data pop, or has the Treasury just proven it cannot cap yields? That is the whole day. The bull framing: the buyback is real, structural demand at $4B clips is new, Wednesday's move was not fake, and a single hot regional survey — Philly Fed is the noisiest of the majors, and its new orders (30.1 from 37.0), shipments (27.7 from 33.7) and prices paid (40.9 from 53.9) all actually fell beneath a headline driven by the diffusion math — does not overturn it. Fed's Daly was on the tape this morning saying she sees no evidence calling for pre-emptive hikes and that policy is in a good place. The bear framing is simpler and harder to argue with: the Treasury spent its most credible non-Fed tool and got one session of relief, and the market's reaction function to that has been to buy gold, silver and Bitcoin rather than duration — which is a debasement vote, not a policy-success vote. The tell to watch is not the level of the 30y but whether it makes a new closing high above 5.28% after an intervention designed to prevent exactly that. If it does, the equity multiple problem is no longer about AI capex financing; it is about the discount rate, and Friday's opex removes the last damping into it. The near-term referee is Chair Warsh at Jackson Hole next week — the minutes revealed a hawkish discussion, and his speech decides whether it becomes a message.

Risk Matrix — Into the Open & Through the Session

1. The 30y closes above 5.28% despite the buyback — an intervention that failed at the level it was built to defend
High
Wednesday's Treasury buyback expansion from $2B to at least $4B, aimed at the 10-to-20 and 20-to-30-year sectors, was the most credible non-Fed tool available for a long end at 19-year highs. It bought one session: the 30y fell about 9bp to 5.19% and is already back to 5.24%, with the 10y round-tripping from 4.65% to 4.70%. If the 30y closes above 5.28% — let alone retests Monday's 5.34% — the market will have demonstrated that direct government purchases cannot cap term premium, and the equity problem stops being about AI capex financing and becomes about the discount rate applied to every cash flow in the index. Wells Fargo said as much on the announcement: short-term relief that does not change the long-term outlook. The counter is that $4B clips are genuinely new structural demand, Daly pushed back on pre-emptive hikes this morning, and Philly Fed's current new orders, shipments and prices paid all fell beneath the hot headline. Watch the 30y close, not the intraday print.
2. WMT's six-year-low comp generalizes and the staples complex re-rates alongside discretionary
High
US same-store sales of +2.6% against +3.8% expected with average ticket at +1.1% versus +3.1% a year ago is the weakest Walmart comp in roughly six years, and it came from the retailer that has been the trade-down winner all cycle. The stock is -8.6% and XLY is -1.6% with XLP -1.0%. The risk is that this stops being one company: if the market decides the consumer is decelerating on spend per trip rather than reallocating across price points, then TGT's +3.8% and TJX's +4% comps get re-read as share gains inside a shrinking pie, and the defensive premium in staples — which is currently carrying part of the index — comes out. The genuine mitigants are specific and substantial: drug-price deflation from Medicare regulations is a mechanical, policy-driven drag unrelated to demand, and Walmart is explicitly recycling 750bps of tariff-refund benefit into lower prices, which suppresses reported comps on purpose. Med

The mitigant is the pattern itself — crude has spiked and faded repeatedly through this war as Saudi cargoes sourced outside the chokepoint absorb the shortfall. But a crude re-acceleration reloads the inflation leg directly into Jackson Hole. XLE is the structural hedge; owning it is not a bullish view.
