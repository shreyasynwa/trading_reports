What Happened Today

How the day actually resolved — and what it hands to tomorrow's open.

1. FLASH PMIs CAME IN HOT AND THE 10-YEAR WENT TO A 19-YEAR HIGH S&P Global's September flash readings blew past consensus at 9:45 AM: services 58.7 against 56.0 expected and 56.5 prior — its best in nearly five years — and manufacturing 56.7 against 53.6 expected and 53.9 prior, a four-year high. The bond response was immediate and parallel rather than steepening: 10Y closed 5.083% (+~12bp), 5Y 4.96% (+12bp), TLT -1.47%, leaving 2s10s roughly where it started near twenty basis points. CME FedWatch moved October hike odds from 57.6% this morning to roughly 70%, on top of the unanimous September 16 move to 3.75-4.00%. Fed Governor Michael Barr said further increases are likely necessary because “risks to achieving our inflation target have increased.” This is the cleanest possible refutation of the disinflation trade: activity is running hotter, not cooling, and the relief the market priced out of cheaper crude never had a demand story underneath it. A parallel shift is a policy-path repricing — it hits long-duration equity and small caps at the same time and cannot be hedged by rotating between them.
2. CRUDE HELD ITS BID AND IRAN RESTATED THE CONDITION THE MARKET HAD PRICED AWAY WTI settled $92.57 (+2.26%) and Brent eased to roughly $98.11 after trading above $101 overnight — both far above the sub-$89 and sub-$99 prints of Tuesday morning. Two things sustained it. An armed group shut a valve on the pipeline out of Libya's El Sharara field, cutting output from roughly 340,000 bpd to about 127,000 bpd — around a third of Libyan production. And Iran's foreign ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei confirmed Tuesday's three-hour Qatari-mediated talks with Witkoff and Kushner but explicitly denied that Tehran had dropped its preconditions, restating that its terms remain an end to the war, the naval siege and the economic war plus release of Iranian assets. Trump called the talks “very productive” with another scheduled, while telling the UNGA he faced a decision between a deal and annihilating the Islamic Republic. Nine dollars came out of crude on a conditional offer; a chunk came back the moment the condition was restated in public. The barrel is a headline instrument in both directions and it is now trading against the equity market rather than for it.
3. THE ROTATION THE MARKET WAS TESTING FAILED THE TEST TWICE IWM closed -1.45%, the worst of the four indices, after leading Monday's pre-market and then leading the downside in Wednesday's futures at -0.61%. QQQ -0.93%, SPY -0.66%, DIA -0.66%. Rate-sensitives took the brunt: utilities -1.65%, real estate -0.95%, consumer discretionary -1.36% with HD -1.83% and LOW -1.67%. Airlines were hit from the other side as fuel held: UAL -2.40%, LUV -2.12%, DAL -1.82%. Energy was the only meaningful bid — XLE +1.62%, OXY +2.65%, COP +2.55%, CVX +1.97%, XOM +1.55%, USO +3.17%. Ten of eleven sectors finished lower. Tuesday's report said to require IWM to hold its relative lead through the close before calling it a rotation, and said the fade would itself be the useful information. The fade was the information: the down-cap bid was a crude headline, not a breadth repair, and it has now been rejected on consecutive sessions.
4. MEGACAP DISPERSION WIDENED AGAIN, THIS TIME TO THE DOWNSIDE GOOGL -3.08% was the largest megacap decliner, with AMZN -1.94%, NVDA -1.78%, AAPL -0.90%, AMD -1.38% and INTC -2.83%. META +2.26% extended the Muse move and MSFT +0.08% held flat. Semis gave back: SMH -1.52%, with SNDK -3.02% and MU -1.85% unwinding Tuesday's roughly +7% and +5%. Pockets of software and security went the other way — CRWD +4.56%, PLTR +3.46%, PANW +2.98% — and IONQ closed +4.39% after announcing a real-time quantum error-correction decoder running on a single standard CPU, having gapped about 11% pre-market. On the downside, APP -5.69% after Edgewater's Joe Wittine flagged that its share expansion has stalled. The Nasdaq lost 1% off a record and the composition of that loss was not uniform. Dispersion cuts both ways and the index level is still the least informative number on the screen. An 11% gap that closes up 4% is distribution, not accumulation.
5. THE DOLLAR, GOLD AND CRYPTO ALL READ THE SAME RATE SIGNAL — CREDIT DID NOT DXY closed 101.07 (+0.47%), near a two-month high, with USDJPY at 158.30 as the yen weakened 0.62%. Gold fell 1.33% to $4,318 and silver 2.24% to $65.04 — a real-rate response, not a haven bid, which is the tell that today was about the discount rate rather than about risk. Copper -1.05%. Bitcoin -2.41% to $84,347, its clearest down day of the week. Credit was the outlier: HYG fell only 0.58%. Four asset classes priced the same twelve basis points and credit declined to participate. That divergence is the strongest remaining argument that this is a rate problem and not a growth problem — and it is the first thing to watch if the 10-year goes through 5.10%.
6. EARNINGS WERE A CONSUMER SPLIT, AND THE AFTER-HOURS DOCKET IS EMPTY Cracker Barrel closed +5.61% on a fiscal Q4 beat — adjusted EPS $0.99 against consensus near $0.26 on revenue of $849.3M (-2.2% y/y, above the ~$845M bar), adjusted EBITDA $62.1M versus $55.7M — guiding FY27 comps +3-5% with no new store openings and roughly 3.0% commodity inflation. Paychex closed -6.73%: adjusted EPS $1.34 beat the $1.32 bar and revenue of $1.63B landed in line, but total expenses ran above plan, adjusted EBITDA missed, and management reaffirmed rather than raised FY guidance. Cintas -0.84% on a one-cent Q1 beat ($1.36 vs $1.35) and revenue of $3.01B (+10.9% y/y) with FY27 guidance of $5.45-5.54 straddling consensus. General Mills -0.54%: net sales $4.4B (-3%), organic flat, adjusted EPS $0.75 (-13% cc), FY27 adjusted EPS guidance of $3.00-3.20 reaffirmed. No S&P 100 name reported after the bell tonight; the next major print is Costco tomorrow after the close. The consumer read is bifurcated — a beaten-down restaurant chain clears a low bar while a payroll processor gets punished for reaffirming. In a 5%-10Y tape, in-line is a sell. Tomorrow's Setup — Three Paths Into the Open

Levels that matter: today's close at $768.38 is the pivot. Above $773 = continuation regime; below $763 = reversal regime; chop in between = pin mode.

Bull path
The PMI is treated as a survey, the 10-year retreats under 5%, and SPY reclaims 773

The constructive path requires the bond market to second-guess itself. The flash composite is a diffusion survey, not a hard datum, and it sits awkwardly against a Richmond Fed manufacturing print of -2 against +5 expected yesterday and a payroll processor guiding flat with rising expenses today; jobless claims at 207K expected against 196K prior is the first scheduled crosscheck tomorrow morning. Credit is already declining to confirm — HYG down just 0.58% on a 19-year-high yield print — and VIX at 14.81 with VIX9D at 13.03 says the options market treats this as a repricing rather than a rupture. Add a Trump-Xi state visit with a Rose Garden review and state dinner attached, and a crude move that is at least partly Libya-specific and therefore reversible on one pipeline headline. Confirmation: the 10-year back under 5.00%, IWM outperforming QQQ on the same session, and SPY closing above 773 with XLU and XLRE participating. The qualifier: nothing on tomorrow's calendar is designed to produce a dovish surprise, and Costco plus PCE sit on the other side of it.

Base case
Chop between 766 and 773 while the tape waits out the summit and PCE

Base case, and what a single-catalyst 0.6% down day into a dense 48 hours usually resolves into. Tomorrow's scheduled US data is second-tier — jobless claims at 8:30 AM and August new home sales at 10:00 AM — with the real events being the state summit during the day and Costco after the close. Working parameters: SPY 766-773 off the 768.63 anchor, 10Y 5.03-5.12%, 5Y 4.92-5.00%, WTI $90-95, Brent $96-101, gold $4,290-4,350, VIX 14.0-16.5. Expect energy to keep the only clean bid, rate-sensitives to stay heavy, and megacap dispersion to keep widening — an index that drifts while its composition churns underneath. Posture: the index print will keep understating what is happening in the components. Hold cheap near-dated gamma across the summit and PCE rather than taking a directional view into a two-event window.

Bear path
The 10-year goes through 5.10%, credit finally confirms, and 762 opens

The bear path needs only continuation. The 10-year at 5.083% is already the highest since July 2007 and roughly 70% odds of an October hike are now priced, with Barr on record that more is likely necessary. A second hot datum — or a firm core PCE Friday — puts 5.15% in play, and there is no comfortable equity multiple there. Crude is now working against the market rather than for it: Libya's El Sharara is offline, Iran has publicly restated its preconditions, and a failed second round of talks returns the full Hormuz premium in one print. The specific thing to watch is credit. HYG fell only 0.58% today; if spreads start confirming the rate move, the current framing of a rate problem rather than a growth problem collapses and the 1.45% IWM decline stops being a rotation story. A second route is the summit itself: Beijing has already refused to help reopen Hormuz and vetoed the UNSC resolution on strait safety, so an energy-security confrontation on Thursday is a repricing nobody is hedged for. Through 766 the tape sees 762, then 760 and 755.
