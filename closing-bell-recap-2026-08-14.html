What Happened Today

What actually drove the tape into the record close — and the after-hours turn that reframes the open.

1. SPX closed at a record 7,798.99 (+0.65%) after tagging 7,816.70 — soft PPI plus a 2% crude slide did the work The index cleared 7,800 intraday for the first time and closed at an all-time high, with seven of eleven sectors green. The fuel was macro, not a single name: July PPI printed flat MoM (vs +0.2% est) and 4.7% YoY (from 5.5%), and crude slid more than 2% (WTI settle ~$81.25, Brent $87.07) as demand worries outweighed the still-curtailed Hormuz transit. The 10y eased to 4.64%. That combination — falling wholesale inflation and a fading energy tax — is the cleanest possible backdrop for the long-duration book, and the rate-sensitive groups took it: Real Estate +1.42%, Communications +2.07%.
2. Memory was the leadership: SNDK +13.7% on an investor-day multi-year model, MU +4.2% SanDisk closed +13.7% at ~$1,528 after laying out a multi-year financial model at its 2026 investor day: mid-to-high-teens annual revenue growth through fiscal 2028-2030. That is an explicit, dated statement that NAND/HBM capital spending stays elevated for years, not quarters — and it dragged the complex, with MU +4.2% and the semicap names (LRCX, AMAT, CAMT) bid into the AMAT print. For the book's DRAM leg this is the corroborating industry signal that the morning framing wanted from AMAT.
3. Breadth confirmed the move without going euphoric — 62% advancing, 76% above the 20DMA Of the tracked universe, 54 advanced vs 33 declined (62.1%), with 75.9% above the 20DMA and 70.1% above the 50DMA; the average member added +0.52%, the median +0.56% — mean and median close together, so the gain was distributed rather than a handful of megacaps dragging the index. The honest asterisk is the small-cap/Dow lag (IWM +0.26%, DIA +0.14% vs QQQ +1.16%): this was a duration-and-semis rally, not a full broadening. SPY's RSI at 67 leaves the index extended but not yet stretched.
4. AMAT reported after the bell — blowout guide, flat margin, stock down ~3% Applied Materials delivered a record quarter (rev $9.12B +25% YoY, non-GAAP EPS $3.50 vs $3.39 est, operating income $3.08B / 33.7%, gross margin above 50% for a 13th straight quarter of expansion) and guided Q4 to $10.25B ±$500M — up 51% YoY against a ~$9.55B FactSet bar — with EPS $4.02 ±$0.20 (+85%), calling AI demand 'unprecedented.' Shares still fell ~3% AH to ~$518. The reason is narrow and specific: Q4 gross margin guides flat sequentially despite the revenue ramp, on ramp costs and a higher display mix — into a stock that had already run ~200%.
5. CALLOUT — VIX9D +20.5% to 16.85 inverted the front end while spot VIX fell The cleanest positioning tell of the day: spot VIX closed down at 14.49 while VIX9D rose 20.5% to 16.85, pushing 9-day implied above 30-day. VIX3M added 5.3% to 20.54, VIX6M 22.28. Dealers are not repricing the regime — they are buying a specific two-hour window: 8:30 AM Retail Sales and 10:00 AM UMich prelim. Read it as event hedging, and watch the unwind: if VIX9D collapses back under spot after 10:00 AM, the vol crush is the tailwind into the weekend; if it stays bid, the tape is telling you the consumer print landed badly.

After-Hours Developments — 4 PM to Now

Three hours of after-hours flow have printed since the cash close. Post-market data not yet populated; refresh after Globex settles at 6 PM..

Two names moved on the tape after the bell, and together they define the trade.
AMAT -3.1% (to ~$518): Q3 revenue $9.12B, +25% YoY (record) and non-GAAP EPS $3.50 vs $3.39 est — both beats — with operating income $3.08B (33.7% of revenue) and a 13th consecutive quarter of gross-margin expansion above 50%. The Q4 guide was the blowout: $10.25B ±$500M, up 51% YoY, against a ~$9.55B FactSet bar, with EPS $4.02 ±$0.20 (+85%) and management describing AI-driven demand as 'unprecedented.' The stock fell anyway on one line: Q4 gross margin guides flat sequentially, on ramp costs and a heavier display mix, into a name that had already run roughly 200%. Implication: the capex cycle is confirmed and accelerating — the market simply refuses to pay for revenue growth that does not convert to incremental margin, which is exactly the discount it applied to CSCO the night before.
SNDK +2.7% (to ~$1,570), after +13.7% in the session: the 2026 investor day laid out a multi-year model of mid-to-high-teens annual revenue growth through fiscal 2028-2030, an explicit multi-year NAND/HBM spend signal; MU closed +4.2% in sympathy. Implication: the memory leg of the AI build-out got its longest-dated confirmation of the cycle, and the tape paid it — the money is flowing to product, not tools.
Net: the AI-capex thesis is not splitting on demand, it is splitting on margin capture. Own the memory-cycle expression; treat semicap strength as beta, not signal. The build's live refresh governs the exact prints.
