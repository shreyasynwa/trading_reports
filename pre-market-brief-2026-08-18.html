Risk Matrix — Into the Open & Through the Session

1. Further Hormuz or Oman escalation puts Brent toward $95 with no SPR left to cap it
High
Trump has declined to revive the memorandum, said he intends to inflict more economic pain on Iran, and threatened to bomb Oman as it reportedly moves toward its own arrangement with Tehran. There is no negotiation to price and no release valve — the SPR is at its lowest level since 1982, so the administration's standard price-suppression tool is spent. Brent above $91 with roughly a fifth of global oil and gas historically transiting Hormuz means the next headline is a step function, not a drift. The compounding problem is that this raises headline inflation, the term premium and the hike odds simultaneously, hitting equities through three channels at once. The counter: crude has repeatedly spiked and faded through this six-month war as covert transits absorb some volume, and a genuine de-escalation would reverse the entire configuration in a session — collapsing crude, letting the long end rally and turning a duration selloff into a violent squeeze. Binary and unscheduled, which is the textbook argument for owning convexity rather than direction.
2. The 10y closes above 4.75% and the AI complex de-rates on the discount rate rather than on earnings
High
The 30-year at 5.31% is already a 19-year high and the 10y at 4.72% has cleared the 4.70% shelf with only 4.75% left before a 19-month high. A close above that level marks down the longest-duration cash flows on the board, which is precisely where index weight is concentrated. The reflexive part is what makes this a risk rather than a mark: the AI capex boom is being funded in the same bond market — NVDA vendor-financing a SoftBank campus, GOOGL weighing a first Australian-dollar bond, AVGO's XPV structure carrying up to $370B of senior debt by 2029 — so the complex being repriced is also supplying the paper doing the repricing. The counter: yields rising on a supply shock rather than on growth are more likely to mean-revert once the shock passes, and yesterday's Anthropic print is a reminder the demand side is genuinely compounding. But demand does not set the discount rate.
3. Wednesday's minutes show the three hawkish dissenters gaining ground into a $91 Brent
High
The last meeting held at 3.50-3.75% on a 9-3 vote with three regional presidents dissenting in favor of a hike, and the market has already moved September odds to roughly 37%. The 2:00 PM Wednesday minutes land into a tape that has just seen Empire State at 20.6, the strongest since 2022, and crude break $91 — the exact two ingredients the dissenters would cite. The divide is whether to look through energy-driven headline inflation at 3.4% or treat it as a threat to the target with core near 2.5%, and a record showing the committee focused on the former reprices a front end still carrying a 15-handle VIX. The counter: the minutes are backward-looking by construction and predate July's weak retail sales, a 51.0 UMich and a soft payroll print, so the market can reasonably discount them in favor of Jackson Hole later this month. Risk is a hawkish surprise into thin near-dated positioning, not the base case.
4. Today's housing starts, permits and TOL confirm the 5.31% thirty-year is already breaking construction
Medium
Housing starts (est 1.390M vs 1.427M) and permits (est 1.380M vs 1.367M) at 8:30, then TOL after the close at 4:30 PM and LOW tomorrow morning. NAHB ticked up to 35, but buyer traffic remains at a deeply depressed 23, and the mortgage complex prices off a 30-year Treasury that just printed a 19-year high. The awkward asymmetry in the current regime is that both outcomes are unhelpful: a soft print confirms the rate damage and hits the builders, cyclicals and LOW, while a firm print adds to the hike case and pushes the long end higher still. Toll is the sharper test because luxury buyers are the least rate-sensitive cohort — if they are hesitating, the damage is broader than affordability math suggests. The counter: HD just reaffirmed full-year guidance with the 30y above 5%, which is real evidence that the renovation channel is holding even as new construction stalls.
5. Friday's opex removes the long-gamma cushion into an unscheduled headline risk
Medium
Dealers still sit long gamma above 770, which is why a -1.11% NQ gap has produced only a -0.51% ES move and why the index has fallen less than the rates move implies. That position rolls off at August monthly expiration on Friday 8/21, and below 770 it inverts from damping to amplifying well before then. Meanwhile the near-dated book is thin — last week's event premium was bought for the retail-sales window and spent rather than rolled — so a 15.88 VIX is buying very little protection into a week carrying FOMC minutes, three big-box guides and an open-ended blockade with no headline schedule. The counter: long gamma is a real cushion right up until the level breaks, and 770 is only about a point below the implied open, so this is more a reason to respect the level than to pre-position for a break. Practically: own defined-risk structures, avoid short premium, and let 770 close before sizing up in either direction.

The skew today is that the bad news is structural and unscheduled while the good news is bottom-up and already known. What broke overnight is not repairable by earnings — Trump refusing to revive the Iran memorandum, Brent above $91 with the SPR at a 1982 low, a 30-year at 5.31%, a 10y through the 4.70% shelf, and September hike odds at ~37% — and none of it has a release time, which means it cannot be hedged around a calendar. What is good is real but small and priced: HD beat and reaffirmed, Empire State printed 20.6, and the consumer thesis that scared the tape last week is looking less fragile than feared. The asymmetry is that the index has given back only about 0.9% from Thursday's record while the long end made a 19-year high, so the equity drawdown has not caught up to the rates move, and a VIX at 15.88 is charging summer prices for an open-ended supply shock. That is not a case for being aggressively short a market whose earnings are fine and whose activity data just surprised to the upside; it is a case for holding direction lightly and owning convexity. Net posture: cut index size, keep Energy as the structural hedge on everything else, express constructive views bottom-up where earnings are doing the work rather than through SPY, treat 770 on a closing basis as the referee and 774 as resistance until proven otherwise, and buy rather than sell optionality into FOMC minutes, WMT and opex 8/21.
