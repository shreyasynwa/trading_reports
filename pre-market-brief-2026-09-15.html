Overnight Developments

The three things that changed between Monday's close and this morning's bell.

1. THE 10-YEAR CLEARED 5% AND HELD — 5.041%, A 19-YEAR HIGH The benchmark 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.041% this morning, its highest level since 2007. Monday's move was a tap-and-reverse — the yield briefly crossed 5.00%, described then as the highest since October 2023, and pulled back. This one has not reversed, and it has moved the reference period back sixteen years. Fed funds futures now imply roughly a 92% probability of a 25bp hike Wednesday from the current 3.50-3.75% target, up from ~86.5% Monday morning and 69.4% Friday. The driver is the same as Friday's hot core CPI print: inflation expectations, with the oil shock feeding directly into the rates story rather than through it. Global equities marked it more honestly than US futures did — Hang Seng -1% to 24,667, Kospi -0.85% to 6,627, ASX 200 -0.88% to 8,672, Topix -0.52%, Nikkei roughly flat at 63,484. A 5%-handle 10-year that does not reverse is the single most important input on the screen, and US equity futures at -0.15% have not priced it. That gap closes one way or the other tomorrow afternoon.
2. SEMIS STABILISED — THE AI-PACING SHOCK HAD NO SECOND DAY After Monday took the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down almost 6% — NVDA -3%, with INTC, AMD and MRVL between -5% and -6% — chips are bid premarket: NVDA +0.6%, MU +0.6%, SKHY +0.4%. The reason is the one flagged yesterday: no major hyperscaler has revised AI capital-spending plans in response to the Amodei essay of September 12 or the Altman and Musk endorsements that followed. Top-five hyperscaler capex is still tracking toward roughly $800B in 2026 and $1.3T in 2027, with Meta guiding $130-145B and Microsoft around $175B. Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid said moderation in the AI investment cycle "seems unlikely" for now. What did persist is the rotation, and it is more precise than Monday's software bid suggested: capital moved out of the buildout chain and into the companies that would profit from spending less — GOOGL +~2%, MSFT +1.6%, META +1.4%. Active managers cutting semis and adding hyperscalers is a considered relative-value view on who captures AI economics, not a flight. Treat the semi complex as a range until a capex number actually changes.
3. CRUDE KEPT GRINDING AND THE YEN GAVE BACK ITS BID Brent climbed 1.6% to ~$107.30 and WTI rose ~1% to above $102, with Saudi Arabia's ~1,200-km East-West pipeline still offline after the September 10 drone strikes from Iraqi territory — infrastructure that routes up to 7 mb/d toward Red Sea ports and serves as the alternative to Gulf shipping under the Iranian blockade. GCC diplomats were expected to meet their Iranian counterpart on a temporary Strait of Hormuz shipping arrangement after Oman's postponement; Houthi forces have advanced to Perim Island. Brent is off Monday's ~$109.13 high but has not given back the shock. In FX, the yen weakened for a second session: USD/JPY rose to ~154.87 from Monday's 153.69 seven-month low as the dollar firmed into the Fed, even with a BoJ hike to 1.25% — the highest policy rate since April 1995 — fully priced for Friday. The yen backing up before a hiking BoJ is dollar strength, not a yen view. It relieves carry pressure into tomorrow and re-arms it for Friday — a two-sided setup, not a resolved one.

Opening Setup — Three Paths Into Cash

Bull path
The 10-year fails back under 4.95%, the semi stabilisation holds, and SPY reclaims 763

The constructive path today runs through the bond market, not the equity market. Confirmation sequence: 10Y under 4.95%, then SPY reclaiming the 759.8 anchor, then a close above 763 opening 765 and 768. Expression: add duration-sensitive exposure only after the yield confirms, not alongside it — the equity market is the follower today.

A 755-763 grind around the 759.8 anchor while desks refuse to size into the SEP

Base case and clearly the most likely. The hike is ~92% priced and today carries no scheduled catalyst — the only session this week that doesn't — so expect an opening range that holds while desks decline to commit ahead of an SEP, a BoE, a BoJ and a quarterly expiration inside four sessions. Working parameters: SPY 755-763 around the ~759.8 anchor, the 10Y 4.95-5.08%, Brent $104-110 with headline risk skewed higher, and front-tenor vol bid into tomorrow rather than spiking. Dispersion remains the dominant feature: the buildout-chain-short, hyperscaler-long expression can run hard while the index prints unchanged. Posture: small in direction, long convexity through 2:00 PM Wednesday, closing-basis levels only, no short gamma into Friday's expiration.

Bear path
5.05% holds on the 10-year, 755 goes, and a hawkish SEP meets quad witching

The bear path does not need an equity catalyst — it needs the yield to stay where it is. A 10-year sustained above 5.05% into a hawkish SEP is a real-rate repricing of the most duration-heavy index composition in two decades, and the cohort that absorbs it first is the same AI complex that took a 6% hit Monday on an unrelated story. The mechanical leg finishes it: a hiking BoJ Friday with USD/JPY having already backed up to 154.9 rather than de-risked, and quad witching pulling dealer inventory 48 hours after the FOMC builds it. Through 755 the tape sees 752 and then 748 with nothing structural between. The tell that this branch is live is the divergence closing downward — flat futures on a 19-year yield high is an unstable equilibrium, and it resolves by equities marking to bonds far more often than the reverse.
