Risk Matrix — Into the Open & Through the Session

1. A tanker casualty or a strike on Kharg Island converts a freight premium into a supply event
High · live, unscheduled, all session
Overnight changed the character of this conflict. The Senegal Prosperity was struck by three projectiles in the strait, a second tanker was attacked, and the IRGC downed a US MQ-9 — attacks on transiting hulls, not on military targets. No casualties have been reported yet, and that word is doing the work: a hull loss or crew casualty is the line that takes Brent through $95 and turns this from a shipping-cost story into a supply story. The escalation path has a named target — Kharg Island handles roughly 90% of Iran's crude exports and Trump has repeatedly threatened it — and a stated ultimatum, that the strait reopens "very soon" or Iran is "hit very hard." Instruction: run no short gamma in energy or in the index through the session; treat Brent $95 as the level that reprices the whole board rather than just XLE. The counter-evidence deserves genuine weight: gold is at a two-week low, bitcoin is steady, there is no safe-haven bid anywhere, Tehran is publicly asking to revive the June deal, and reporting is consistent that neither side is seeking full-scale war.
2. The 10-year at 4.79% is a term-premium move, and long-duration equity has no hedge against it
High · structural, builds all week
The 10-year at 4.79% is the highest since January 2025, and it happened while September hike odds on FedWatch fell from 61.9% to roughly 56%. That combination rules out the comfortable interpretation. This is not the market pricing one more twenty-five basis points; it is the market demanding more compensation to own duration at all — energy-driven inflation risk, a war-risk premium and supply, arriving together. The failure mode is specific and expensive: buying long-duration equities on a dovish FedWatch headline, when the discount rate doing the damage is not the one moving. It is already visible — NQ -1.2% against ES -0.6%, SMH -1%+, NVDA/AMD/MU each down more than a percent, while small caps are off only about half a percent. Instruction: watch the 10-year through 4.85% as the level that forces the next leg of the equity mark-to-market, and demand the equal-weight tape rather than the index level as confirmation of any bounce. Do not size a rate trade off a single venue — 49% on Polymarket, 56% on FedWatch, 68% market-implied is a dispute, not a price.
3. DELL tonight pre-positions the SOX badly into AVGO Wednesday
High · after the close, resolves Wednesday
Dell carries roughly a $28.9 billion implied swing into tonight's print and has the precise setup that has broken four consecutive AI-hardware reports: strong server demand meeting component costs that eat the margin guide. The comparables are unambiguous — HPQ -11% on an outright beat, MRVL -10% on a 90bp sequential step-down, NVDA guiding to a 71-72% Q4 gross-margin trough on memory. Dell closed $456.24 against a $510.26 consensus target with no sell ratings, which means the sell side is positioned for a good print and the options market is not. If the margin guide disappoints, the SOX enters AVGO Wednesday — the last top-20 name to report and the only one that can generalize or break the memory-cost de-rating — already impaired, with semis down more than a percent before the bell today. Instruction: no long semis exposure into Wednesday's close that you would not hold through a 5% gap. Screen hardware on gross margin, software on disclosed AI monetization. Do not sell semiconductor premium assuming the earnings crush has happened — a margin de-rating does not decay on schedule.
4. Vol is still cheap and there is no gamma cushion under a tape that is now gapping
Med · structural, all week
VIX closed 14.13 on Friday — the lowest print of 2026 — and has barely moved through a military escalation, a shipping attack, a cycle-high 10-year and a 0.6% futures gap. The seasonal median from here runs toward 18 by mid-September and 19 in early October, so the tape is paying below the historical median into a week containing ISM today, DELL/PANW tonight, the Beige Book and AVGO Wednesday, ISM Services Thursday and the August employment report Friday. There has been no meaningful dealer long-gamma rebuild since August expiration, which means no mechanical damping in either direction — a break travels rather than stalls, and so does a relief squeeze on a benign prices-paid print. Instruction: own front-week SPY and QQQ convexity through the September 4 payroll report and do not sell it to finance an intraday position. Treat any vol compression on a soft ISM as an add rather than a signal.
5. Idiosyncratic strength gets mistaken for breadth on a gap-down open
Med · today specifically
This morning's green names are all stock-specific and none of them say anything about the tape. Robinhood is up more than 2% on a Morgan Stanley upgrade to overweight; Novartis is up 4% on multiple-sclerosis trial data; XLE is up more than 1% because crude is up, not because risk appetite improved. Meanwhile the actual structure of the session is NQ -1.2%, SMH -1%+ and a 10-year at a cycle high. The mirror-image error is equally available: reading an ISM-driven bounce off 762 as evidence that the yield move is over, when a single August survey collected largely before the tanker attacks cannot settle that question. Instruction: require the equal-weight tape green and the 10-year back under 4.75% before treating any reclaim of 767 as structural, and read every level on a closing basis. The two moments that carry information today are 10:00 and the close. Everything between them is positioning.

The skew tightened overnight because the downside acquired a third trigger while the upside acquired a real one for the first time in a week. The bear side needs any of: a tanker casualty or a move on Kharg Island taking Brent through $95; an ISM prices-paid re-acceleration from 73 at 10:00 that converts a 4.79% 10-year from term premium into policy and pushes yields through 4.85%; or a soft DELL margin guide tonight that pre-positions the SOX into AVGO Wednesday — and 762 is already being tested at the bell rather than approached, with 759 and 757 beneath and no gamma cushion in between. The bull side is genuinely available and specific: a third consecutive cooling prices-paid print validates Goldman's warning against hawkish bets, backs the 10-year off 4.79%, and repairs the most-damaged part of the tape fastest precisely because NQ is down 1.2% purely on duration. What keeps this two-sided rather than one-way is the cross-asset evidence, which remains stubbornly calm: gold at a two-week low near $4,440, bitcoin steady at $78,340, no safe-haven bid anywhere, no reported casualties in either tanker attack, Tehran publicly seeking to revive the June deal, and September hike odds on FedWatch actually falling to roughly 56% from 61.9%. What keeps it asymmetric is the book: VIX started this from 14.13, the lowest close of 2026, and no dealer long-gamma has been rebuilt since August expiration — nothing damps this tape in either direction. Net posture into the open: hold front-week convexity through September 4; run no short gamma in the index, in energy, or in AI hardware into DELL tonight and AVGO Wednesday; trade the prices-paid line at 10:00, not the ISM headline; express the geopolitics through energy and the yield move through avoiding duration, not through index direction; and demand the 10-year back under 4.75% before treating any 767 reclaim as structural. The levels that arbitrate: 767 is the reclaim, 762 must hold on a close, and beneath 759 there is nothing before 757.
