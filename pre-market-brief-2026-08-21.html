Risk Matrix — Into the Open & Through the Session

1. The ROST gap gets sold into the expiration close and the consumer rebuttal dies with the tape up
High
The bull case now rests on gaps holding, and there are three specific reasons they might not. First, composition: roughly $0.60 of ROST's $2.66 EPS is tariff refunds — precisely the non-recurring quality this tape punished in TGT Wednesday and inside WMT's own 750bp operating-income benefit Thursday. A market that sold both cannot consistently pay full price for the third. Second, valuation: the stock enters at a high-20s multiple after running from $208 in late June to $255, so a +9% gap has almost nothing to lean on. Third, mechanics: expiration Friday is the single worst day to trust a gap, because dealer flows dominate price discovery and only the settlement counts. BJ's partially inherits the same problem — its headline +11.9% comp is gas-inflated and the +3.1% ex-gas number is what a skeptical desk will mark it on. The counter is real: +10% comps driven by traffic with a 6–7% guide against a 3% Street is not accounting, membership fee income +9.9% is recurring, and TJX and TGT traffic sit on the same side. The discriminating observation is free: watch whether ROST closes above its opening print. If it fades on a green day, the consumer complex stays a source of supply into Jackson Hole no matter what the numbers said.
2. A hot prices-paid PMI at 9:45 puts the 30y back at 5.30% with the policy backstop already spent
High
This is the largest risk because the mitigant has already been tested and it broke. The Treasury announced it would double long-end buybacks to $4B+ per operation from Sept 9 through Nov 4, Bessent said publicly that "yields don't reflect the underlying fundamentals" and that he is ready to expand further — and the market returned the 10y to 4.71% and the 30y to ~5.24% within a session and held it overnight. There is no untested tool left between the long end and a hawkish outcome. The specific trigger is 9:45 AM's flash services PMI, and the line that matters is prices-paid rather than the headline: input costs were already elevated in July with supplier delivery times deteriorating at one of the fastest rates in four years, and crude is bid on the Iran campaign. A hot print pushes the 30y toward the 5.30% 19-year high with the buyback already discounted and Warsh at Jackson Hole 8/28 holding every incentive to validate it. The counter: buybacks do genuinely absorb duration and the program has not started — September 9 is the real test, not this week's tape. But do not run a book that requires the long end to be capped.
3. The Iran waiver expires today and the mechanics publish Monday — the scope question sits over an unhedgeable weekend
High
The calendar is the risk. The 60-day full waiver allowing Tehran to sell crude lapses today; Bessent publishes the isolation mechanics Monday; and the intervening 65 hours are unhedgeable for a cash equity book. Trump has promised the "most crushing economic operation ever taken against any country" under Operation Economic Fury and threatened penalties on any nation helping Iran evade sanctions — aimed at China, which buys the bulk of Iranian crude and has already cut imports to ~534k bpd in August from ~823k in July. The physical buffer is gone: Hormuz transits ran 73 in the week ended Aug 16 versus 91 prior, far below pre-war norms, and the SPR sits at its lowest since 1982 with no release capacity. A broad secondary-sanctions regime reaching Chinese refiners is not priced. What makes it systemic rather than sectoral is the interaction: an oil shock landing on a long end at 4.71%, six days before a Fed chair speaks, is the one combination neither the Treasury nor the Fed can absorb — one lever fights inflation, the other fights yields, and they point opposite ways. The mitigant that has capped this three times is Saudi supply sourced outside the chokepoint. XLE is the hedge; owning it is not a bullish equity view. Size before today's close.

Bottom Line

Index playbook (SPY/QQQ structural): SPY/QQQ: treat the 762–766 band as the base case and the close as the only signal that counts. Expiration pins — intraday breaks today are noise, and chasing an up-open into decaying open interest is how books give back gains on the third Friday. The reclaim trade is a settlement above 766, which puts 770 — the gamma line — in play next week; the failure trade is a settlement below 762 on a green tape with ROST +9%, BJ's beating and crypto at its best week since 2023, which would be the strongest bearish tell available because it means the bid could not survive good news at a 4.71% 10-year. QQQ at $710.93 is the weaker vehicle: its five-day losing streak is a rates story, and rates have not improved, so an NQ +0.5% open is short-covering and consumer sentiment rather than duration relief — fade strength there before fading it in SPY. Prefer IWM over SPY only if small caps convert a green open into a green close, which is the clean confirmation that the DXY at 98.8 liquidity bid is real. Keep index size small into the settlement and carry 30-day convexity through 8/24, 8/26 and 8/28 rather than expressing a directional view today.

Single-name playbook (intraday options): Intraday options: the highest-quality free signal on the board is whether ROST closes above its opening print — that is the referendum on whether this tape will pay for a beat containing ~$0.60 of tariff refunds, and it reads across to the entire consumer complex. Express the consumer relatively: XLY and off-price over XLP, not directionally. BJ's is a two-sided setup into the print reaction — the +9.9% membership fee income argues one way and the +3.1% ex-gas comp the other, so let the first thirty minutes decide rather than pre-positioning. WMT is a stabilization watch, not a dip-buy; the pharmacy-cap drag is legislated and recurring. XLE is the Hormuz hedge and it carries a same-day event in the waiver expiry — own it as insurance, not as a bullish equity view, and be sized before the close rather than Monday morning. In semis, no directional size and no short premium before NVDA on 8/26; if constructive, express it in analog and equipment rather than memory or optics, and note CoreWeave's Hudson River Trading deal as a demand-side datapoint that does not answer the cash-flow-quality question. Crypto proxies — MSTR, COIN — are the highest-beta way to be wrong about the debasement trade after a 20%+ week that is itself a source of Monday supply.

Into the open: the green line is ROST holding its +9% gap into the close, a contained prices-paid PMI that keeps the 10y under 4.75%, XLY leading XLP, IWM converting a green open into a green close, and SPY settling back above 766. The red line is ROST gapping up and closing below its opening print, a hot prices-paid print pushing the 30y toward 5.30% with the buyback already discounted, or SPY settling below 762 into 758 on an up-futures open with two retail beats and crypto at its best week since 2023 — that last one is the worst of the set, because it means the tape could not hold a bid on genuinely good news, it happens with the gamma cushion dissolving at the bell, and Monday opens on Bessent's Iran mechanics the session after the waiver expired, with nothing underneath it.
