Overnight Developments

What changed between Thursday's close and this morning's bell.

1. BoJ HIKED TO A 31-YEAR HIGH AND THE YEN FELL ANYWAY The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate 25bp to 1.25%, the highest since 1995 and the fastest tightening pace since 1990, citing the risk that inflation deviates above 2%. The vote was 7-2, with board members Toichiro Asada and Ayano Sato dissenting. The yen weakened as much as 0.8% to 157.145 per dollar — its weakest since September 3 — and the Nikkei rose more than 1%, with JGB yields falling. The read from the desk was blunt: the split vote underwhelmed, and the market took two dissents as evidence the sequence is closer to done than to starting. This is the most important thing that happened overnight, and it happened in the risk-positive direction. The globally-important carry trade unwind that RSM and others flagged into this meeting did not begin — it got deferred.
2. BUFFETT STEPS DOWN AS BERKSHIRE CHAIRMAN Warren Buffett, 96, announced in a shareholder letter Friday that he is stepping down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, the roughly $1T conglomerate he has run since 1965, becoming chairman emeritus effective immediately while remaining on the board. His son Howard Buffett becomes chairman; Greg Abel, 64, has been CEO since taking over roughly nine months ago; Susan Decker stays lead independent director. “Father Time always wins,” Buffett wrote. “He has, however, been generous with me.” Long-telegraphed and structurally priced — the succession plan has been public since May 2025. Expect a headline-driven BRK.B print rather than an index event, and expect it to land on the highest-volume tape of the quarter.
3. CRUDE DOWN A THIRD DAY WHILE THE WAR FRONT WIDENS Brent fell 2.2% to ~$102.53 and WTI 1.8% to ~$100.04, with US crude briefly dipping below $100 — a third consecutive decline and Brent's first weekly loss in three, down about 2%. The drivers are logistical: Saudi Arabia is working to return roughly half the East-West pipeline's capacity within days, is offering more cargoes to Asian refiners via ship-to-ship transfer off Oman's Sohar port, and product inventories built in the US, Singapore and Europe. Against that, Saudi Arabia and the Houthis exchanged fresh cross-border strikes Thursday, and the Trump administration approved a potential $24.3B sale of 48 Lockheed Martin F-35s plus 49 Pratt & Whitney engines to Riyadh as the Houthis push a ground offensive toward Bab el-Mandeb. LMT +0.2% to ~$539 premarket. PVM's Tamas Varga: the current fundamental outlook would not justify a prolonged fall below $100 on Brent. Treat sub-$100 as an overshoot into a weekend with an expanding conflict, not a new level.
