Base case is what is already happening. Posture: small in direction, long front-week convexity, and let 4:20 PM do the work.

NVDA misses the FQ3 guide or the margin, the software discount jumps a layer, and 766 fails with no cushion beneath it

Bear path has one primary trigger and one aggravator. Primary: NVDA clears the revenue line — which is nearly automatic with a 1% gap — and then disappoints on the thing that actually matters, either an FQ3 guide that only meets or a gross margin compressed by the doubled DRAM cost it has been passing through. Bottom Line

Index playbook (SPY/QQQ structural): SPY and QQQ are a range trade until the bell and a gap trade after it. The cash band is 766-770 and it has held all session without conviction in either direction — trade inside it, do not chase either edge, and treat 766 as a closing line rather than an intraday one. The asymmetric thing to watch is an afternoon push toward 770: taking it on a close is what flips dealer hedging from amplifying to damping and would be the only cushion this tape gets before the print, which makes it worth more than the four points suggest. QQQ is the split index — semis bid, software broken — so its flatness is manufactured from two large sectors disagreeing, and it will resolve violently rather than gradually. Size for that: with no dealer long gamma since August expiration, a 7% NVDA move against 7.3% index weight is roughly 50bp of SPY overnight, which is 766 to 762 in one gap, before correlated selling in AMD, MRVL, SMCI and memory. Hold semis, stop adding at a repaired price. Do not buy the software gap before 4:20 PM — if NVDA validates, IGV is still available Thursday at a better-informed price, and if it does not, today's software damage was the early warning.

Single-name playbook (intraday options): Intraday options: the front week is the position and everything else is expression. Own weeklies spanning tonight and 8/28 outright and finance them from nothing — that structure covers both remaining binaries and it is priced as though only one exists. Run no short gamma in semis, software or the index into the close, under any framing, including covered calls on semis you intend to keep. On NVDA specifically: implied 5.4-7% against a ~7.6% twelve-quarter average is cheap in absolute terms, but recognize what you are buying — with a 1% guidance gap the revenue line carries no information, so the distribution is bimodal around the FQ3 guide and gross margin, and the unmodeled upside is China compute revenue the guide zeroed out. That argues for owning both tails rather than a directional strike. Avoid selling premium in AMD, MRVL (which reports Thursday), SMCI or memory as an NVDA proxy — the correlation goes to one on the gap and the vol crush you are counting on does not arrive. In software, do not sell puts into today's damage before the print; in discretionary (KSS, WSM, ANF tonight), stay a funding short against quality rather than a bounce buyer.

Into the close: the green line is SPY closing above 770 — which would restore a damping mechanism before the gap — with software stabilizing rather than accelerating lower and semis holding their bid; then an NVDA print that defends gross margin against doubled DRAM costs and signals China compute revenue the guide had zeroed out. The red line is SPY losing 766 on a close heading into the print, or an NVDA FQ3 guide that only meets — because with a 1% guidance gap the revenue beat proves nothing, and a soft guide would confirm exactly what software priced today: that AI is a cost being absorbed somewhere, and if not by the application layer's customers then by the hardware layer's margin. With no dealer long gamma since August expiration, a 7% move on 7.3% of index weight is roughly 50bp of SPY overnight — 766 to 762 in one gap, 758 the trapdoor with nothing between it and 754. The mispricing did not get cheaper today, it got denser: 15.5 VIX now covers two binaries instead of six. Own the front week outright and run no short gamma into the close.
