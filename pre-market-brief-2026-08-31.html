Risk Matrix — Into the Open & Through the Session

1. Hormuz escalates from a contained exchange into a supply event and crude prices the tail
High · live, unscheduled, all session
Sunday's exchange was contained — eight missiles intercepted over Jordan, no casualties — but the trigger that produced it was IRGC crews preparing to fire sea mines into the Strait of Hormuz, and that intent has not gone away. Roughly one-fifth of world oil shipments normally transit the waterway; current flow is 6-8 million barrels a day with vessels reportedly paying tolls on a limited Iranian-approved route. Trump has warned any vessel laying new mines will be "immediately and systematically destroyed," and Bessent has committed to weekly secondary sanctions starting with regime-linked banks — an escalation cadence with a schedule. Brent has already swung nearly $17 in range this month. Instruction: do not carry short-gamma exposure in energy or in the index through the session, and treat Brent through $95 as the level that converts this from an equity headwind into a Fed decision. The counter-evidence to respect: gold is lower, DXY is flat, bitcoin is unchanged — cross-asset is not pricing a crisis, so do not chase the first spike.
2. The energy impulse takes September through 70% and forces the equity mark-to-market that Friday deferred
High · builds through Thursday's ISM Services
September is 61.9% priced, from 41.4% a week ago, and Barclays just moved from zero hikes in 2026 to two. A sustained crude bid is the fastest possible route above 70% because energy transmits into headline inflation within weeks, and the chair has already said PCE at 3.7% y/y has not convinced him underlying inflation improved. The equity complex has not marked any of it: ES -0.24% against a 2-year that jumped 12bp Friday to its highest close since January 2025. The mark-to-market comes through the 10-year above 4.80% and lands hardest on the most rate-sensitive expressions — the Russell, already under its 50-DMA, and the leveraged mid-cap complex. Instruction: demand the equal-weight tape as confirmation of any rally, not the index level; a green SPX with the average stock lower on month-end is rebalance mechanics, not demand.
3. Vol is the cheapest thing in the room and there is no gamma cushion under the tape
High · structural, all week
VIX closed 14.13 Friday, the lowest print of 2026, and is only bid to 15.28 this morning on a live military exchange and a 3.87% crude move. The seasonal median for this date is near 16.5, rising toward 18 by mid-September and 19 in early October — so the tape is paying below the historical median into a week containing ISM, the Beige Book, AVGO, ISM Services and the August employment report. There has been no meaningful dealer long-gamma rebuild since August expiration, which means there is no mechanical damping on either side: a directional break travels rather than stalls, and the index's recent stability has come entirely from rotation rather than from structure. Instruction: own front-week SPY and QQQ convexity through September 4 and do not sell it to finance a month-end position. Treat any VIX compression into today's close as an add. Do not sell semiconductor premium assuming the earnings crush has happened — what is repricing there is a margin de-rating, which does not decay the way the model says.
4. AVGO Wednesday generalizes the memory-cost de-rating across the semiconductor complex
Med · Wednesday after the close
Four consecutive AI-hardware prints have beaten on demand and been marked down on unit economics: NVDA guiding gross margin to 74.0% with a 71-72% Q4 trough on memory, HPQ -11% on an outright beat, and MRVL -10.28% on a 90bp sequential step-down. AVGO is the last top-20 name to report and the single largest test of whether this is a company problem or an industry one. The complicating evidence is that MU is up roughly 13% in August and SK Hynix is reportedly evaluating Intel Foundry for HBM base-die production — the market is paying memory pricing power at the same time it is penalising memory cost. Both readings cannot survive Wednesday. Instruction: no long semis exposure into Wednesday's close that you would not hold through a 5% gap, and use DELL and PANW Tuesday plus HPE Wednesday as the surrounding evidence.
5. Rebalance flow is mistaken for demand and the day's close is read as a signal
Med · today specifically
Today has no data that matters and no earnings that matter. That makes the entire session a flow event, and month-end flow has a known and mechanical direction: August closed with the index up and the average stock lagging, so the rebalance points toward the laggards into the last hour. The failure mode is reading a rebalance-driven bounce as evidence that the tape has absorbed the Hormuz strike and the 62% September print — it has not, it has simply not been tested. The mirror-image error is reading an opening gap-down as a regime break when ES is only -0.24% and there is no safe-haven bid in gold, the dollar or crypto. Instruction: discount everything before 3:00 PM, require the equal-weight tape green before treating 772 as structural, and size the close for flow rather than for a view. Tuesday's ISM is the first honest read of the week.

The skew is unusually clean this morning: the downside has two independent triggers and the upside has one mechanical flow. The bear side needs either a Hormuz escalation — mine-laying, a tanker casualty, or an exchange with casualties, taking Brent through $95 — or simply for crude to hold $86 into the close, which alone pushes September past 70%, takes the 10-year through 4.80%, and puts 766 on a closing break with only the rising 20-DMA before 762 and 760. The bull side needs the exchange to stay contained and month-end rebalance demand to buy the laggards into the last hour — real, mechanical, and worth respecting, but it is one day of flow against a repricing that has run for four sessions. What makes it asymmetric rather than merely two-sided is the book: VIX started at 14.13, the lowest close of 2026, is only at 15.28 on live military headlines, and no dealer long-gamma has been rebuilt since August expiration — nothing damps this tape in either direction. The constructive evidence deserves equal weight and is genuinely there: Jordan intercepted all eight missiles with no casualties, gold fell 0.56%, DXY is flat, bitcoin is unchanged, the Venezuela agreement is a real supply-side offset, and August still closes green with the Dow up 2.1% for a fifth straight monthly gain. Net posture into the open: hold front-week convexity through September 4; run no short gamma in the index, in energy, or in AI hardware into AVGO Wednesday; demand the equal-weight tape green before treating 772 as structural; express the geopolitics through energy and rates rather than through index direction; and size the close for rebalance flow rather than for a view. The levels that arbitrate: 772 is the reclaim, 766 must hold on a close, and beneath 762 the 20-DMA stops being support.

Opening Setup — Three Paths Into Cash

Bull path
The exchange stays contained, oil fades the spike, and month-end rebalance flow buys the laggards into 772

The bull path does not need the hike priced out — it needs the Middle East to stay a headline. The evidence for that is already in the tape: Jordan intercepted all eight missiles with no casualties, gold fell 0.56%, DXY is flat at 99.6 and bitcoin is unchanged — there is no safe-haven bid anywhere in cross-asset, which is what a contained exchange looks like. Add the supply-side offset: the Venezuela agreement covering 65B+ barrels of proven reserves is a genuine medium-term bearish input for crude, and 6-8 million barrels a day are still moving through Hormuz. If oil gives back half the 3.87% during the cash session, the inflation impulse deflates and the front end stops adding. From there the mechanics take over: August closed with the index up and the average stock lagging, so rebalance flow points toward utilities, industrials and the Russell into the last hour, and SPY works 772, then 776 and 780. Confirmation is specific and non-negotiable: a close above 772 with the equal-weight tape green and crude off its highs. Expression: buy the funding sectors rather than the leaders, keep the front-week convexity on regardless, and do not treat an oil-driven energy-sector rally as index breadth.

Posture: small in direction, long the front week, respect 766 and 772 on a closing basis only, and let the calendar do the work.

Bear path
Hormuz escalates or crude holds the bid, September goes above 70%, and 766 fails with no gamma cushion beneath it

The bear path has two independent triggers and only needs one. Trigger one is the waterway: a successful mine-laying, a tanker casualty, or a second exchange with casualties takes Brent through $95 and makes the September inflation print a foregone conclusion. Roughly one-fifth of world oil shipments transit Hormuz, and Bessent has committed to weekly secondary sanctions — this is a conflict with a scheduled escalation cadence, not a one-off. Trigger two needs no new news at all: if crude simply holds $86 into the close, the energy impulse is real, September prices through 70%, the 10-year takes out 4.80%, and 766 goes on a closing basis. The aggravator is positioning: VIX started this at 14.13, the lowest close of 2026, and no dealer long-gamma cushion has been rebuilt since August expiration — there is nothing on the book to damp a move, so it travels rather than stalls. The internals are already thinner than the index: the Russell is under its 50-DMA, SPX names down more than 3% ran to 45 from 20 on Friday, and the index only held because megacap absorbed a 3.5% SOX drawdown. If that bid stalls there is no second line, and it runs straight into AVGO Wednesday and Friday's employment report.
