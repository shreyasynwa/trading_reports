Regime
Wednesday was a textbook case of the decision carrying no information and the language carrying all of it. The FOMC voted 12-0 for 25bp to 3.75-4.00% — the first hike since July 2023 — and all three indices were higher at 2:00 PM. The selling started with the press conference. Warsh: “I would be hard pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive. This view was widely shared by the committee, so we removed a dose of accommodation.” That is a materially different framing than Powell's “modestly restrictive” — it says 3.50-3.75% was not high enough, and by implication 3.75-4.00% may not be either. He declined to say whether policy is restrictive now, distanced himself from the SEP — “those aren't my forecasts, those are the forecasts of my 18 colleagues” — and said “I'm not in the forward guidance business.” The statement itself asked for a “timelier return” to 2%. The dots: 16 of 18 participants see at least one more hike this year, with 2027 split 8 hike / 6 hold / 4 cut. Warsh has submitted no dot since May. Close: SPX 7,551.81 (-0.45%), Dow 51,462 (-1.21%), Comp 25,978.42 (-0.01%) — financials led the Dow decline, and the dollar index rose 0.6% to its strongest since late July. Overnight the crude leg reversed hard on politics rather than logistics: Trump said the U.S. is “hopefully toward the end” of the war and has spoken with Tehran “directly,” layered on top of Saudi ship-to-ship loadings off Oman's Sohar port. WTI -1.7% to ~$100.70, Brent -2.1% to ~$103.60 — Brent has now round-tripped roughly $4.50 off Tuesday's high. Cross-asset: 2Y ~4.72% after touching a 2024 high, 10Y holding near 5%, gold firm around $4,310-4,330 (+1.0%). MU +2.9% to ~$953.54 leads the premarket tape; SPCX +1.8% to ~$153.65 on Cathie Wood's $10T Starship revenue call.
Key binary
Today's binary is whether the oil collapse is allowed to do the disinflation the Fed just said it would not wait for. The case for the bounce: Brent at ~$103.60 and falling is the single cleanest input into the second-round-effects problem Warsh explicitly named, the 2-year already eased to ~4.72%, futures are green, and an actual end to the Iran war re-rates the entire energy-inflation complex rather than one barrel. The case against: the 10-year is refusing to leave 5% even with crude down 2%, which says the long end is pricing a chair who thinks rates are too low rather than an oil shock — and a hawkish Fed paired with falling oil is a growth-negative combination, not a relief one. Fed funds futures now show traders split on whether the next hike comes in October, which is the widest genuine uncertainty this cycle has carried. Confirming instruments, in order: the 10-year through 4.92% is the tell that the oil fade is being read as disinflation and the bounce is real; the 10-year holding above 5.00% on a 2% crude decline means the bond market is marking the chair, not the barrel, and the equity bounce is a fade; SPY reclaiming 757 on a closing basis with better-than-55% breadth confirms; SPY through 749 puts 745 in play with quad witching 24 hours out. Overnight Developments

What changed between Wednesday's 2:00 PM statement and this morning's bell.

1. TRUMP SAYS THE WAR IS “HOPEFULLY” NEARING ITS END — AND HAS SPOKEN TO TEHRAN DIRECTLY “Well, hopefully we are toward the end of the war. They want to make a deal, we'll see how that works out,” Trump told reporters in North Carolina Wednesday evening, and said he had spoken with Tehran “directly” without elaborating. It is the seventh month of the conflict. Crude took it immediately: WTI -1.70% to ~$100.70, Brent -2.07% to ~$103.60, extending a fade that began on Saudi ship-to-ship loadings off Oman's Sohar port and the U.S. Energy Secretary's “measured in days” call on the East-West pipeline. The ground reality has not improved in parallel: Yemeni government forces are fighting Houthi advances near the Kahbub mountains by Bab al-Mandeb and around Taiz, with Saudi air support. If this is real, it is the largest single disinflationary input available to this Fed — and it arrived less than 24 hours after the chair said he cannot influence oil but must prevent its second-round effects.
2. THE FRONT END EASED, THE LONG END DIDN'T — 2Y ~4.72% WITH 10Y STILL NEAR 5% The rate-sensitive 2-year note fell about one basis point to ~4.72% after climbing to its highest since 2024 in Wednesday's post-FOMC session. The 10-year held near 5%, a day after touching 5.04% — the highest since July 2007 — and has not participated in the crude fade. The dollar index rose 0.6% Wednesday to its strongest since late July. The mechanical read: the front end is pricing a policy path that is now genuinely two-sided — fed funds futures show traders split on an October hike — while the long end is pricing a chair who will not call 4% restrictive. A 10-year that won't fall on Brent -2% is the single most informative price on the screen. Until it moves, treat the equity bounce as positioning rather than repricing.
3. FUTURES BOUNCED WITH SEMIS LEADING — MU +2.9%, SPCX +1.8% Dow futures +0.2%, S&P 500 +0.3%, Nasdaq 100 +0.5% on Thursday, reversing part of Wednesday's post-presser slide. MU is the most active name premarket, +2.9% to ~$953.54, with the company's last quarter having printed revenue up 346% year over year to $41.5B and Q4 guidance of ~$50.0B at ~86% gross margin — its report lands September 30, after this expiration. SPCX rose 1.84% to ~$153.65 after Cathie Wood argued Starship could generate $10T in annual revenue by 2030 at 10,000 flights a year, a target Musk called “not impossible”; the next Starship test is September 22. The bid is concentrated in duration-sensitive growth, which is unusual 18 hours after a chair refused to call policy restrictive. Semis leading on a hawkish repricing is the tell that this is a crude-driven bounce, not a rate-driven one. It only holds if the 10-year follows the barrel.

Risk Matrix — Into the Open & Through the Session

1. The chair has removed the ceiling on the terminal rate and won't replace it
High
Warsh said he would be “hard pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive,” described the hike as removing “a dose of accommodation,” declined to say whether 3.75-4.00% is restrictive, disowned the SEP as “the forecasts of my 18 colleagues,” and said he is “not in the forward guidance business.” That is a chair explicitly declining to define neutral while running policy toward it. The market response was not about the hike — indices were green at 2:00 PM — it was about the width of the resulting distribution, and equities pay for width. Fed funds futures now split on October, with Oxford Economics arguing markets have too much tightening priced and LPL arguing no cut until 2028. Instruction: stop trading this Fed off the dots. Own convexity rather than direction, and treat every scheduled Fed appearance between now and the October meeting as a repricing event that requires no data to fire. Size positions to survive a 15bp move in the 10-year on a speech.
2. The 10-year refused to fall on a 2% crude decline
High
Brent fell 2.07% to ~$103.60 and WTI 1.70% to ~$100.70 overnight, and the 10-year is still pinned near 5% after Tuesday's 5.04% — the highest since July 2007. Instruction: the 10-year is the scoreboard today, not SPY. Under 4.92% the crude fade is being read as disinflation and the equity bounce has legs.
