TL;DR — What You Need To Know

THE RECLAIM HAPPENED, THE LEADERSHIP DID NOT. SPY settled $766.06 — precisely this morning's bull pivot — on SPX +0.43% to 7,674.31, DJIA +517.60 (+0.98%) to 53,276.81, COMP +0.43% to 26,180.46, RUT +0.88% to 3,018.81, VIX -5.81% to 15.08. But the Dow and the Russell doubled the S&P's gain while the technology sector fell a sixth straight session and IT finished the week down over 3%. Every index still posted a weekly loss, the S&P roughly -1.4%. This was repair on rotation, not a bid for the index's largest constituents.
THE BEST MACRO PRINT OF THE WEEK MOVED THE LONG END EXACTLY ZERO. Flash services PMI jumped 2.2 points to 56.8 — a 20-month high versus a 54.0 Street and 54.6 prior — with manufacturing light at 53.2 vs 53.9, and the price components cooled: input costs at their slowest since February, selling prices the softest since November. The morning's stated bear trigger was a hot prices-paid line; it printed the opposite, and the 10y still closed 4.71% with the 30y near 5.25%. A long end that will not rally on benign inflation is repricing fiscal supply and AI debt issuance, not CPI — which is why the buyback round-trip never repaired.
ROST FAILED THE ONE FREE TEST. The stock opened roughly +8% and closed +5.8% — below its opening print, which was this morning's explicit discriminator. The consumer numbers were real (comps +10% traffic-led, Q3 guided +6–7% vs ~3%, gross margin +625bp), but roughly $0.60 of the beat was tariff refunds and the tape marked it down accordingly, the same way it marked TGT and WMT. Share shift into value channels is confirmed; the market's willingness to pay a full multiple for it is not.
THE CUSHION IS GONE AND MONDAY IS LOADED. Expiration settled the dealer long-gamma structure that has sat above 770 all month — Monday is the first session of August with no damping, and it opens on Bessent's Iran isolation press conference, the session after the 60-day oil waiver expired, with WTI $86.72 and Brent ~$93.6 on a second straight weekly gain. Meanwhile Bitcoin closed ~$77,065, +6.1% on the day and ~+22% on the week — its best in two years, gold $4,680 (+2.4%). A 15.08 VIX into that calendar is the cheapest thing on the board.
NEXT WEEK IS THE WHOLE QUARTER IN 72 HOURS. Bessent Monday, NVDA Wednesday after the close alongside July PCE (core expected 3.3% y/y), Q2 GDP second estimate and durable goods, then Jackson Hole opening Thursday with Warsh's first keynote as chair Friday morning on Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy — a topic sitting directly on top of Bitcoin's best week in two years. Do not carry short premium through it.

Tomorrow's Setup — Three Paths Into the Open

Levels that matter: today's close at $765.53 is the pivot. Above $771 = continuation regime; below $761 = reversal regime; chop in between = pin mode.

Bull path
Bessent's Iran scope lands narrow, SPY takes 770 back, and dealers return to damping

Bull path into Monday: Bessent's mechanics exclude Chinese refiners or arrive with enough carve-outs that crude stays capped near $86–88 WTI, and the market treats the waiver expiry as already priced. The good macro then gets a second look — services PMI at a 20-month high with input costs at a six-month low is a real print, and if the 10y can trade back under 4.65% on it, the long-duration complex finally gets relief it has not had since July. SPY clears 770 on a close, which puts dealers back into damping mode and turns hedging flows from amplifying to absorbing for the first time since the last expiration cycle. 774 and 778 become the magnets into NVDA Wednesday. In this path the tech sector's six-day losing streak ends on rates rather than on news, semis catch a bid ahead of the print, and the broadening that showed up Friday — Dow +0.98%, RUT +0.88% against SPX +0.43% — persists rather than reverses. Expression: IWM over SPY, XLY and off-price over XLP, and keep XLE on as insurance even in the bull case because you are paid to be wrong about Hormuz.

Base case
Post-opex drift in 762–770 with the tape waiting for NVDA and Warsh

Base case: Monday gaps on the Bessent headline, finds no follow-through in either direction, and the week compresses into a holding pattern between 762 and 770 until Wednesday evening. This is the classic post-expiration profile — the structure is gone, so ranges widen intraday, but nothing forces a resolution while the two events that matter are still ahead. The 10y holds 4.65–4.78% with fiscal supply and buyback anticipation fighting to a draw into the September 9 first operation. Crypto consolidates a 22% week rather than extending it, which removes Friday's leadership without replacing it, and the index goes quiet on declining volume — exactly the low-liquidity August tape Bessent himself described as the problem. The tech sector's streak stops on exhaustion rather than on a bid. Posture: this is the week to be small in direction and long in convexity. Own the 30-day window spanning 8/24, 8/26 and 8/28; do not sell the front to finance it, because the front is where the gap risk actually lives.

Bear path
Secondary sanctions reach Chinese refiners, crude breaks $95 Brent, and SPY loses 762 with no cushion

Bear path: Bessent's Monday mechanics reach Chinese refiners, the scope the market has not priced. Brent breaks $95, WTI clears $90, and an oil shock lands on a long end already at 4.71% with the SPR at a 1982 low and no release capacity — the one combination neither the Treasury nor the Fed can absorb, since one lever fights inflation and the other fights yields. The 30y goes to 5.30% on the inflation impulse, the tech sector's six-day slide becomes eight, and the crypto complex that led Friday gives back a chunk of a 22% week, removing the only leadership the tape had. SPY loses 762 into 758 with 754 beneath, and this is the specific configuration where the damage compounds: with the long-gamma structure settled away, hedging flows amplify rather than damp, realized correlation — which has been doing all the work suppressing a 15.08 VIX — normalizes off the level break, and index vol catches up in a single move rather than over a week. NVDA on Wednesday then reports into a market that is already short duration and long fear, which is how a fine print gets sold.

Bottom Line

The session story: The market cleared the bar it set for itself this morning and did it with the wrong names. SPY settled $766.06 — the exact bull pivot — as the S&P rose 33.15 points (+0.43%) to 7,674.31, the Dow added 517.60 (+0.98%) to 53,276.81, the Nasdaq Composite gained 113.29 (+0.43%) to 26,180.46 breaking a five-day losing streak, the Russell 2000 rose 0.88% to 3,018.81, and VIX fell 5.81% to 15.08. Every index still finished the week lower. The macro was better than anyone asked for: flash services PMI jumped 2.2 points to 56.8, a 20-month high against a 54.0 Street, with manufacturing light at 53.2 and — the part that mattered — input costs at their slowest pace since February and selling prices the softest since November. This morning's bear trigger was a hot prices-paid line; it printed the opposite, and the 10-year still closed 4.71% with the 30-year at 5.25%. That is the third time in a week the long end has refused a reason to rally, and it moves the diagnosis from inflation to supply. Underneath the green index, the composition was a rotation rather than a recovery: energy, health care, financials and crypto-linked beta led — HOOD +13.70% to $108.13, TSLA +5.14% to $362.86, PLTR +3.44%, with Bitcoin +6.1% to ~$77,065 for a ~22% week, its best in two years, and gold +2.38% to $4,680 — while the technology sector fell for a sixth consecutive session and IT closed the week down more than 3%. The consumer got its verdict too, and it was a qualified one: ROST opened roughly +8% and closed +5.79%, below its opening print, which was the single free datapoint this morning named — the tape paid for +10% traffic-led comps and a +6–7% Q3 guide but discounted the roughly $0.60 of tariff refunds inside the beat, exactly as it discounted TGT and WMT. The afternoon rally halved on thin volume before stabilizing, and the after-hours session — with no Mag-7 or S&P-top-10 name reporting — offered no follow-through at all. Then the bell rang on August monthly expiration, settling away the dealer long-gamma structure that has sat above 770 for the entire month. That is the handoff: an index that reclaimed its level on borrowed leadership, a long end that will not cooperate on any input, a tech complex being de-rated on financing, and a Monday that opens with no cushion into Bessent's Iran mechanics.

Into Monday's open: the green line is a narrow Bessent scope that spares Chinese refiners with crude capped near $86–88 WTI, SPY holding 766 through the headline and reclaiming 770 on a close, the 10y trading back under 4.65% so the tech complex's six-day slide ends on rates, and the Russell keeping its relative bid. The red line is secondary sanctions reaching Chinese refiners with Brent through $95, the 30y pushing 5.30% on the inflation impulse, the crypto complex giving back a piece of a 22% week and taking Friday's only leadership with it, or SPY losing 762 into 758 — and that last one is the worst of the set, because it is the first uncushioned session of the month, hedging flows now amplify rather than damp, and a 15.08 VIX resting on unusually low realized correlation catches up in a single move rather than over a week.
