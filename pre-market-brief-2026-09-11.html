Risk Matrix — Into the Open & Through the Session

1. The 10-year takes 5.00% and the equity/bond divergence resolves toward the bond market
High
Friday produced fresh 52-week highs at every single tenor — 2Y 4.63%, 3Y 4.712%, 5Y 4.775%, 7Y 4.859%, 10Y 4.959%, 20Y 5.377%, 30Y 5.346% — on the same session equities rallied 0.86%. The two cannot both be right. The front end is the tell: the 2-year moved 8bp on a headline print that came in exactly as forecast, which is the market moving its terminal expectation rather than next Wednesday's probability, and core CPI at +0.3% m/m against a +0.4%/5.4% PPI gives the committee cover to validate the ~60% October lean. Mortgage rates hit 7% inside 48 hours, so the transmission is already running. Instruction: use 5.00% on the 10-year as the arbiter on every equity decision this week. Above it, treat Friday's bounce as a lower high and long-duration equity as the wrong side. Back under 4.85% and the bounce is real with 767 and 770 live — but nothing scheduled before Wednesday can deliver that, so do not anticipate it.
2. A fully-priced FOMC has no upside surprise and real downside surprise in the dots
High
CME odds for Wednesday's quarter-point hike sit at roughly 86-90%, up from under 70% before the CPI print, with October follow-on near 60%. That pricing removes the decision as a tradable event and moves the entire risk into the projections and the statement. If the committee's language or dots validate October — and Regan Capital's read that "we may see several rate hikes over the coming months" is not a fringe view — the front end has room to reprice violently from 52-week highs. The offsetting argument exists: LPL's Roach makes the case that transmission is weaker this cycle, with nominal growth above 6% and AI investment plus affluent consumption insensitive to rates — but that is a medium-term argument, not a Wednesday-afternoon one. Instruction: own front-week SPY and QQQ convexity through the 16th and do not finance it by selling anything expiring on the 18th. Size direction small into the decision. Treat any statement language opening October as the single largest repricing risk of the week.
3. Two hiking central banks 48 hours apart, then quad witching the next morning
High
The Fed decides Wednesday the 16th; the BoJ is expected to take policy to 1.25%, a roughly 31-year high, on the 17th-18th; September quad witching lands Friday the 18th. USD/JPY is at 154.17 with the yen up more than 3% on the month and near seven-month highs, driven by BoJ tightening expectations, carry-trade unwinding and capital repatriation. A funding currency appreciating into a hiking Fed is a deleveraging mechanism rather than a macro talking point: leveraged positions close regardless of thesis, and the cohort that pays is whatever is most crowded — the narrow megacap and AI complex carrying this index. Then the quarter's largest expiration removes whatever dealer inventory is pinning price, hours after the second decision. Instruction: carry no leverage across the 16th-18th. Track USD/JPY as a risk instrument: through 152 the unwind has resumed and the correct response is reducing gross, not hedging delta. Above 156 it was positioning and the calendar can be traded on its own terms.
4. Oil re-escalation from a supply picture that never improved
Med
Brent fell 3.6% to ~$104 after touching $108 Thursday — the highest since May 19 — and that pullback is what Friday's equity bounce was bought on. But Houthi forces seized the port of Mocha and now threaten Bab el-Mandeb transit, Reuters-verified satellite imagery showed smoke near Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, the Jizan refinery was struck again, Saudi output fell roughly 1.9 mb/d in August, and Trump said he has "no regrets" and sees the war continuing past the November midterms. Bessent's pre-announced Monday sanction on "a large bank" adds a financial-channel escalation on top. The asymmetry is the problem: another infrastructure headline puts Brent back through $108 and repeats the four-day slide from a higher yield base, while further crude weakness delivers progressively less equity relief because the rate expectation it created does not un-price. Instruction: size index exposure against Brent holding under $100, not against Friday's close. Keep energy as the cheapest hedge on the board. A Bab el-Mandeb transit headline is the single most valuable thing to watch on Monday.
5. The tape has stopped paying for AI demand and started charging for its financing
Med
Oracle beat on every line — $1.92 adjusted EPS against $1.74, $19.35B revenue against $19.14B, cloud revenue +62% to $11.6B, infrastructure +121%, backlog ~$664B — opened about 6% higher near $163, and round-tripped the entire move into negative territory by Friday afternoon. The reason is structural: capex of $28.50B against $8.50B a year earlier, roughly $125B of debt, negative free cash flow of $5.4B, and the stock down 48% over twelve months. Meanwhile the hardware sellers were the day's leadership — HPE +11.6%, DELL +10%, HPQ +8% — and SpaceX added 1.25% on a ~$13B annualized compute agreement. That split is the discount-rate story the 52-week-high curve is telling, expressed in single names. Instruction: read the fade rather than the beat. If a print that good cannot hold a gap with the 10-year at 4.96%, assume no AI-levered name holds one into or out of Wednesday. Favour capex beneficiaries over leveraged capex spenders while the curve makes highs, and carry no single-name binary across the FOMC-BoJ pair.

The asymmetry into Monday is created by the calendar and by what Friday's rally did not fix. On the upside, a reclaim of 767 requires Brent holding under $100, the 10-year failing at 5.00%, and Wednesday's statement declining to validate the ~60% October lean — three things that must go right together, with nothing scheduled before the FOMC able to deliver any of them and the hike itself 86-90% priced, so there is no upside surprise left in the decision. On the downside, a single Red Sea or Saudi infrastructure headline puts Brent back through $108 and repeats this week's four-day slide from a 4.96% starting yield; the 2-year moved 8bp on an in-line print, which says the terminal expectation is repricing, not just the meeting probability; a BoJ hike to a 31-year-high 1.25% lands 48 hours after the Fed with USD/JPY already at 154.17; and September quad witching removes whatever dealer inventory is pinning price the next morning. Oracle round-tripping a 6% gap on a clean beat is the cleanest evidence of what this tape now pays for good news. The correct expression is convexity rather than direction: own optionality through the 16th-18th, size direction small, use 5.00% on the 10-year and 152 on USD/JPY as the two confirming breaks, and reduce gross rather than hedging delta if the yen goes.
