What Happened Today

The four things that turned a four-day slide into one broad green close — and the one thing that did not change.

1. CRUDE BACKED OFF $105 AND THAT, NOT THE CPI PRINT, BROKE THE FOUR-DAY SLIDE WTI fell 3.35% to ~$99.05 and Brent 3.61% to ~$103.70, unwinding the move that produced Thursday's fourth consecutive down day when Brent tagged $105. Capital.com's Kyle Rodda framed the week's action as "uncomfortably similar to the volatility experienced at the start of the U.S.-Iran war," with Thursday's ~15bp jump in the 2-year to a two-year high and a sharp plunge in precious metals as the knock-on. President Trump said he has "no regrets" about the Iran war and sees it running past the November midterms. The supply-side facts did not improve: Houthi forces hold Yemen's port of Mocha, tightening their grip on the Bab el-Mandeb, and Reuters-verified satellite imagery showed smoke near Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline. The pullback is positioning, not resolution — and it is the entire foundation of Friday's tape.
2. AUGUST CPI: IN LINE ON THE HEADLINE, A TENTH HOT ON CORE, AND THE HIKE WENT TO ~86% CPI rose 0.4% m/m and 3.4% y/y, both matching the Dow Jones consensus. Core rose 0.3% m/m — 0.1pp above forecast — with the core annual rate at 2.4%, down from 2.5% and in line. Energy drove the uptick: fuel costs +28% y/y, diesel +52%, with technology, airfares and communication also contributing. CME FedWatch moved to ~85.6% for a 25bp hike Wednesday, from roughly 70% before the print. Nationwide's Kathy Bostjancic said rates could stay on hold "only if disinflation continues and today's August report did not deliver that." Regan Capital's Skyler Weinand went further — "a rate hike next week is all but assured... we may see several rate hikes over the coming months." This was the last inflation input before the meeting. The hike is priced; the path is not.
3. FRESH 52-WEEK HIGHS PRINTED AT EVERY TENOR — ON A DAY EQUITIES ROSE 0.9% 1Y 4.341% (+7.7bp), 2Y 4.63% (+8bp, a two-year-plus high), 3Y 4.712% (+6.5bp), 5Y 4.775% (+4.2bp), 7Y 4.859% (+2.7bp), 10Y 4.959% (+1.5bp), 20Y 5.377%, 30Y 5.346% — every one a 52-week high. The shape is the information: the front end moved roughly five times the long end, which marks a policy-path repricing rather than a term-premium or supply event. Gold fell 0.66% to ~$4,404 and silver rose 0.37% to ~$65.17. Mortgage rates hit 7% inside 48 hours during the same move. Equities rallying into a curve setting fresh highs at every point is a divergence that resolves, not a coexistence that persists. The 2Y is the arbiter.
4. BREADTH AND LEADERSHIP WERE GENUINELY BROAD — AI HARDWARE AND REFINERS BOTH WORKED Nine to ten of eleven S&P sectors closed higher, with Technology, Industrials and Communication Services each better than +1% and Health Care and Utilities lagging. The single-name tape: HPE +11.6% extending a post-earnings re-rating on AI server, networking and storage demand; DELL +10% on an RBC Outperform initiation citing free cash flow and AI-infrastructure scale — it joins the S&P 100 on September 21; HPQ +8% on an RBC Sector Perform initiation with a $33 target; GOOGL +2.66%; AAPL +2.23%. Energy, the year's leading sector at roughly +42% YTD, saw refiners VLO, PSX, PBF, MPC, DINO and DK all print 52-week highs even with crude down 3%. Losers were idiosyncratic: STX -3.1% on multiple-driven profit taking, CLX -3.1% on a Barclays target cut, ALB -2.5% ex-dividend, and the small-modular-nuclear names SMR and OKLO among the day's worst. Refiners at 52-week highs on a down-crude day is a crack-spread signal, not a beta signal — the cleanest sector tell of the session.

Bear path
A weekend crude headline or hawkish dots take 761, and quad witching removes the cushion

The bear path needs only one of three things. First, crude: Houthi forces hold Mocha and the Bab el-Mandeb, satellite imagery showed smoke near the Saudi East-West pipeline, Trump says the war runs past the midterms, and Bessent sanctions a large bank Monday — a Brent close back above $105 reinstates the exact configuration that produced four straight down days.
