Risk Matrix — Into the Open & Through the Session

1. The global bond rout extends through 4.85% and there is no equity hedge against a term-premium repricing
High · structural, builds all week

That rules out the comfortable read. This is not one more 25bp being priced; it is the world demanding more compensation to own sovereign duration, with fiscal supply, an energy-driven inflation impulse and a war-risk premium arriving together. BlackRock is explicit that "the global reset in interest rates has further to run" and "higher yields are here to stay for a reason" — and they are tactically overweight equities while saying it. The failure mode is buying the dip in the leveraged end of the market on the assumption that a soft labour print buys rate relief: ADP at 38K bought none, and the Russell paid 1.23% for it at the open. Instruction: treat the 10-year as the confirming instrument for every equity decision today. 4.85% forces the next leg of mark-to-market; 4.75% says the rout is exhausting. 3. A soft payroll print that does not move September pricing is the stagflation outcome

If Friday repeats that pattern, cyclicals and small caps take the growth hit with no discount-rate offset, and there is no rotation available — utilities, one of only two green sectors Tuesday, are simultaneously being broken by California's SB 492. Instruction: own the front week through Friday. Do not position for a dovish repricing on soft data until the front end actually corroborates it.

Opening Setup — Three Paths Into Cash

Bull path
Crude keeps fading, the 10-year backs off 4.75%, and the Beige Book reads soft enough to put a dent in 68%

The bull path is already partly running, and it starts with the fact that crude faded on an escalation day — Brent $95.19 to $94.28, WTI to $89.58 — because physical supply is still reaching the market. If that continues, the bond rout loses its proximate driver, and the 10-year backing under 4.75% from 4.814% is the single event that repairs the most damaged part of the tape fastest. The second trigger is the Beige Book at 2:00 PM: a regional picture that reads soft on hiring — corroborating ADP at 38K, manufacturing -17K, professional services -16K and Uber cutting 10% of its workforce — is the first thing that could put a genuine dent in 68% September pricing. The third is AVGO after the close, where Dell's beat-and-raise on AI servers has already removed the worst tail and a clean $16B AI semiconductor print with confident FY27 framing re-rates the whole complex into next week. The supporting evidence is real: gold at $4,355 and falling, silver -1.7%, bitcoin near $76,700 — there is no safe-haven bid anywhere, which continues to argue this is a discount-rate event rather than a crisis. Mechanically, the Russell took -1.23% purely on duration, so it repairs first. Confirmation: a close above 765 with the 10-year under 4.75% and the Russell green. Expression: buy what the yield move sold — small caps and the rate-sensitive complex — not the energy names that the oil move bought.

Index chops 759-765 on rotation, the Russell keeps paying the bill, and the real verdict waits for AVGO tonight and payrolls Friday

Base case and the most likely. The gap was small (SPY 759.90 against Tuesday's 761.80), the open was mixed (SPX +0.06%, Dow +0.37%, Nasdaq -0.06%, Russell -1.23%), and crude fading took the urgency out of it. So the tape holds its configuration: the index chops 759-765 on violent single-name dispersion, the Russell underperforms while the Dow holds up, the 10-year sits 4.75-4.85%, Brent holds $92-96, gold stays heavy near $4,355, and bitcoin holds $76,000. Vol stays bid but does not break out: VIX has come off a 14.92 base to above 16 after tagging 16.80, which is a repricing rather than a panic, and the seasonal path from here runs toward 18-19. Then the week's actual risk arrives in order: AVGO, HPE and SNOW tonight, ISM Services Thursday, the August employment report Friday. Posture: small in direction, long the front week, closing-basis levels only, and let the calendar do the work.

Bear path
The global bond rout extends through 4.85%, or AVGO disappoints on AI growth, and 759 fails toward 754

The bear path has three independent triggers and needs only one. First, the rout: this is a synchronized global move — gilts, bunds, OATs and JGBs together, the JGB near a multi-decade high — and synchronized moves have their own momentum independent of the news that started them. A 10-year through 4.85% forces the next leg of equity mark-to-market, and there is no equity-complex hedge against a term-premium repricing. Second, the waterway: two tankers have struck naval mines, Iran has hit US bases in Jordan, the UAE and Kuwait, and Trump says he is "not trying to force Iran to the bargaining table." A crew casualty, a hull loss, or an actual closure takes Brent through $98, and Hansen's $5-in-either-direction range means that repricing happens in hours. Third, tonight: AVGO is guided to a ~74% gross margin on TPU mix and is therefore being judged on the $16B AI semiconductor line and the FY27 framing — a growth wobble there is far more damaging than a cost miss would have been, and it lands the same day Lutnick confirmed a semiconductor tariff framework is coming. Beneath 759 the map is 757 then 754. The aggravator is structural: no meaningful dealer long-gamma has been rebuilt since August expiration, so a break travels rather than stalls. And note where the vulnerability already sits — the Russell at -1.23% and utilities broken by California legislative risk means both the leveraged end of the market and one of its two defensive hiding places are already impaired before Friday's payroll print.
