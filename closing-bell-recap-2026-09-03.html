What Happened Today

One Fed governor, one conditional sentence, and a 623-point Dow — plus the three things that sentence did not touch.

1. Waller took September to a coin flip — hike odds 63.2% → 50.4%, 10-year 4.818% → 4.75%, Dow +623 Speaking at a Reuters event, Waller said he is "willing to sit and be patient," that the market should "give disinflation a chance," and that "we can wait one meeting" — expecting a reasonable CPI, flagging a possible significant drop in core once revisions land, and describing AI-driven disinflation as a growing force. That matters because he is a governor: July's 9-3 hold saw Hammack, Kashkari and Logan dissent for a hike — the most dissents since 2016 — and not one governor joined them, so a governor signalling patience is the committee's center of gravity moving, not a regional outlier. Fed funds sit at 3.50-3.75% and FedWatch now prices September 15-16 at 50.4%. Into tomorrow, the whole repricing is conditional on data he has not seen — which means an in-line payroll print holds it and a hot one voids it in a single session.
2. The bid went to duration and beta, not to breadth — Russell +0.51% against SPX +1.06% Eight of eleven sectors closed green with advancers leading 15:8, but the leadership table is a rate trade end to end: Consumer Discretionary +1.29%, Financials +0.98%, Technology +0.75%, against Materials -0.21%, Health Care -0.28%, Consumer Staples -0.30%. TSLA +7%, HOOD +15%, SNOW +20.8% on its second straight blowout (product revenue +37%, cRPO $4.91B vs $4.75B) carried the high-beta complex, and gold +2.84% to $4,539.90 plus bitcoin near +4.6% confirmed the softer-dollar, softer-yield signature. The Russell's +0.51% is the tell: small caps are the cleanest read on genuine rate relief, and they took barely half the index move. Into tomorrow, treat today's leaders as the highest-beta expression of a 50.4% coin flip — they are the first thing repriced if payrolls run hot.
3. AVGO fell ~4% after raising FY27 AI revenue to $115B from $100B — the guide is still the only thing that matters Broadcom lifted its FY27 AI revenue outlook to $115B from $100B and told the street it doubles again to roughly $230B in FY28 (10GW to 20GW), naming Anthropic and OpenAI as its largest FY28 customers, each exceeding GOOGL — and the stock fell about 4% because Q4 revenue guided to $34.8B against $35.03B with gross margin 66% vs 66.5% expected. The AI capex thesis is not what is being sold here; the next-quarter number is. That pattern ran all week — PANW -7.8% and MDB -12% on beats — and it ran again tonight in LULU and GWRE. Into tomorrow: stop trading beat headlines and screen the forward guide, and do not read AVGO's decline as an AI demand break, because the FY27/FY28 revisions went the other way.
4. Vol got sold into an event — VIX broke to the mid-14s with payrolls at 8:30 and the market shut Monday VIX closed near 14.6, down roughly 4%, taking out the 14.92 base it came off Monday and sitting well under the seasonal median near 18 for mid-September. That happened on a day when the market bought a conditional Fed signal and did nothing at all about Brent $95.52 after four up sessions, record $5.820 diesel, or ISM prices paid at 72.6. No meaningful dealer long-gamma has been rebuilt since August expiration, so there is no mechanical damping in either direction. Into tomorrow: an 8:30 payroll print, a pre-holiday session with thinned liquidity, and a three-day weekend is the single worst configuration in which to be short front-week convexity.
The market did not learn today that the Fed will hold. It learned that one governor will hold if August inflation cooperates. Everything priced since 10:00 AM is collateral against a number nobody has seen.

Tomorrow's Setup — Three Paths Into the Open

Levels that matter: today's close at $765.16 is the pivot. Above $770 = continuation regime; below $760 = reversal regime; chop in between = pin mode.

Bull path
Payrolls print near +53K with contained wages, the 10-year holds under 4.75%, and the Waller repricing becomes a trend

The bull path needs one thing tomorrow: a payroll print that lets Waller's conditional stand. Near +53K consensus with wage growth contained is the sweet spot — soft enough to keep hike odds at or below 50.4%, firm enough to avoid the growth scare. If that lands, the 10-year holds under 4.75%, the September binary starts resolving toward a hold, and the market gets to extend a session it has already validated: Dow +623.57 to 53,685, its best since August 4, on genuinely improved rate arithmetic rather than positioning. The supporting evidence is better than the bearish framing allows. ISM services new orders hit a three-and-a-half-year high at 60.9 and business activity ran 61.7 — that is real demand, not a stall. AVGO raised FY27 AI revenue to $115B from $100B and guided FY28 to roughly $230B with Anthropic and OpenAI as its largest customers, which is the capex cycle extending, not ending. SNOW +20.8% with product revenue +37% and cRPO $4.91B, IOT +16% on 30% ARR growth and PATH +13% say enterprise software demand is accelerating. And claims at 206,000 with continuing claims at 1.779M is not a labour market in distress. Confirmation: a close above 776 with the Russell green and the 10-year under 4.75%, opening 779 and 782. Expression: if the print is friendly, the trade is the broadening — IWM and the equal-weight tape over more QQQ, because small caps took only half of today's move and have the most to recover.

Base case
A pre-holiday 770-776 chop as the market refuses to extend a coin flip into a three-day weekend

Base case and clearly the most likely. The setup is a market that has already taken its repricing and has no incentive to press it before a 72-hour close. SPY settles near 773 after a +1.06% S&P session, sitting exactly on the upper shelf flagged this morning at 770-773, with hike odds at 50.4% — the most genuinely undecided level possible — and the deciding data still ahead. Expect the 8:30 payroll print to produce a first-hour move that gets substantially faded by noon as pre-holiday desks flatten, leaving SPY chopping 770-776, the 10-year 4.72-4.82%, Brent $93-98, USDJPY 154-157, and dispersion staying extreme with IOT +16% and GWRE -19% on the same overnight tape. The internals argue for the same conclusion: the Russell managed only +0.51% today and Fear & Greed still reads 35 despite a 623-point Dow, which is not a market preparing to chase. Posture: small in direction, long front-week convexity through the print, closing-basis levels only, and let the September 15-16 FOMC be the event rather than tomorrow's tape.

Bear path
A hot payroll number re-arms September, the 10-year takes back 4.81%, and today's leadership unwinds into a weekend nobody can hedge

The bear path is simply the arithmetic of a conditional running in reverse, and it needs only one number. Waller explicitly conditioned his patience on incoming inflation data; hike odds are 50.4%, not 20%; and a payroll print materially above the +53K consensus with firm wages re-arms September in a single session and voids a repricing the market has already capitalized at full value. The mechanical path back is short: the 10-year sits 4.77% against a 4.818% high printed 24 hours ago, so a retest is one datapoint away, and the leadership that would reverse hardest — Consumer Discretionary +1.29%, Financials +0.98%, Technology +0.75%, plus SNOW +20.8%, HOOD +15% and TSLA +7% — is exactly the cohort that rallied on rate relief. The worse version is the stagflation print: a weak payroll number landing on ISM prices paid at 72.6, new orders at a three-and-a-half-year high, Brent $95.52 after four up sessions and record $5.820 diesel. That combination takes cyclicals and small caps down while denying the rate offset, and the Russell's +0.51% today shows the small-cap complex was never really participating. The aggravators are structural and specific to tomorrow: a pre-holiday session amplifies any 8:30 surprise on thin liquidity; no dealer long-gamma has been rebuilt since August expiration, so a break travels rather than stalls; VIX at 14.6 means nothing is pre-hedged; and the market is shut Monday for Labor Day, so whatever the tape holds at 4:00 PM Friday it holds for three days. Beneath 770 the path is 767 then 765, and September's seasonal record — down 55% of the time since 1928, averaging -1.1% with the damage concentrated in the second half — offers no help.
