Overnight Developments

The four things that changed between Friday's close and this morning's bell.

1. OMAN SHELVED THE HORMUZ TALKS AND THE SAUDI PIPELINE STAYED SHUT — BRENT CLEARED $108 Omani Foreign Minister Sayyid Badr Albusaidi said late Sunday that Monday's GCC-Iran meeting on a Strait of Hormuz shipping proposal had been postponed "in the interests of consensus," with no new date set — the near-term de-escalation catalyst simply removed. Saudi Arabia's roughly 1,200-kilometre East-West pipeline, the main route to Red Sea ports that bypasses the strait, remained offline through the weekend after the September 10 drone strikes, which Riyadh attributed to militants in Iraq. Traders told Reuters that stored volumes at Yanbu cover exports for five to seven days; beyond that window as much as 4% of global supply is exposed. Brent opened ~$108.36, ranged $108.04-$109.13 and last traded ~$108.15; WTI opened ~$102.83, ranged $102.57-$103.65. Brent had already gained almost 9% last week. Houthi forces claimed a large ballistic-missile and drone attack with "direct hits" on King Khalid Air Base at Khamis Mushait, and captured Perim plus the Greater and Lesser Hanish islands, tightening their hold on the Bab al-Mandab. Capital.com's Daniela Hathorn: "Oil is once again the biggest geopolitical story... the oil shock is feeding directly into the rates story." IG's Tony Sycamore flagged that absent something operational from the talks, oil "continues to extend its gains towards the $119.48 high" of early March. This is a supply story with a clock on it. The Yanbu window is the level to watch, not the price.
2. THE AI INDUSTRY CALLED FOR ITS OWN SLOWDOWN AND SEMIS ENTERED A BEAR MARKET Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay over the weekend urging frontier AI companies to slow the pace of model-capability development on safety grounds. OpenAI's Sam Altman followed — warning development could go "very badly," that humans could lose control, and that "we are unapologetically on Team Humanity" — and Elon Musk backed the call, all inside 72 hours. The trigger behind it was a researcher resignation, with the departing researcher saying the people building AI "earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade." President Trump dismissed the call Sunday, saying a slowdown was not needed and would jeopardise America's lead over China. The tape: an ETF tracking major chip stocks fell ~4.6%, SOXX -6% against QQQ -2%, and the group now sits ~20% below its June high. LRCX -8% in a semi-equipment rout, MRVL -7.6%, AMD down 6-7%, INTC -7%, with NVDA, MU, SNDK and SK Hynix all lower. TheStreet Pro's Stephen Guilfoyle: "This is going to at least start out ugly," expecting memory, hyperscalers, high-end designers and semi-cap to lead lower. No hyperscaler has changed a capex plan in response. Until one does, this is a multiple repricing on sentiment, not a demand shock — which is exactly why it is a gap to fade rather than chase.
3. CAPITAL ROTATED INSIDE TECH RATHER THAN OUT OF IT — AND THE RUSSELL IS GREEN The rotation is the cleanest read available this morning. Winners: CRWD +5.35% after CEO George Kurtz answered Amodei directly, saying frontier labs will keep building regardless of individual company decisions and that cybersecurity's role is to make that development safer; PANW +5.10% on a strong fiscal Q4 beat and continued AI-security demand; NOW +4.68% as investors moved toward names seen as less exposed to an AI-development slowdown. Losers outside semis were idiosyncratic: GLW -9.16% on a new equity offering of up to $2B and the dilution it implies, and SPCX -2% to $148.16 on the AI complex plus continuing post-IPO lockup releases — David Einhorn used Greenlight's Q2 letter to call the $1.75T IPO valuation a marker that a "speculative top is near," even as SpaceX flew its 700th Falcon mission Sunday. Energy names bid with crude, XOM among them. And the Russell 2000 is +0.45% while the Nasdaq Composite is -1.17%. Software up 5% on the same headline that took semis down 6% is a relative-value trade, not a risk-off. Require the Russell to turn red before treating this as an index-level event.
4. THE CURVE HELD ITS HIGHS, METALS BROKE, AND CRYPTO IGNORED ALL OF IT The 10-year is little changed near 4.96-4.99% after tapping the 5% psychological marker on Friday — still its highest since October 2023. CME FedWatch puts Wednesday's 25bp hike at ~86.5%, up from 69.4% Friday morning, with a further hike increasingly priced before year-end. The Fed has held 3.50-3.75% since the December 2025 cut, so this would be Chair Warsh's first move. Gold fell 1.76% to ~$4,331.40 and silver 2.86% to ~$63.33 — both lower on a day with a contested Bab al-Mandab and a shut Saudi pipeline, which only happens when real-rate expectations are overwhelming the haven bid. Bitcoin went the other way: +1.9% since midnight UTC to as high as ~$78,280 from a $76,806 open, sitting out the AI selloff entirely. USD/JPY is near 153.7, a seven-month low, with OIS implying ~75bp of cumulative BoJ hikes by April 2027. Equities, gold and silver falling together with the 10Y pinned at a two-decade high is a discount-rate tape. Nothing before Wednesday 2:00 PM can resolve it.
