What Happened Today

Friday, August 28 — the events that drove the tape into a month-end Monday.

1. Warsh went hawkish and named AI capex as a source of inflation In his first Jackson Hole keynote as chair, Kevin Warsh called AI a "hinge point in history" and said he expects it to raise output and lower costs over time — then said that in the near term the massive investment in AI data centers is itself contributing to higher inflation, driving up the cost of construction and memory chips. On policy: "While this summer's readings were better than expected, they do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved," and "We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do." He said rates showed little evidence of being restrictive, called the breadth of inflation concerning, and defended his "quieter Fed" project: "We should not indulge a regime in which market participants are looking primarily to the Fed for their next trade." This is the first time the AI build-out has been described from that podium as a reason rates may need to go higher rather than lower.
2. The front end repriced two hikes by March — the largest post-Jackson-Hole 2-year move this century The 2-year Treasury yield jumped 12 basis points to 4.38%, the highest close since January 2025 and, per JP Morgan data, the largest 2-year reaction to a Jackson Hole chair speech this century. It now sits roughly 73bp above the effective fed funds rate, the widest since November 2022. The 10-year rose 5bp to 4.73%, its second-highest close of the year; the 30-year reversed early gains to close +2bp at 5.21% — a bear flattening across the curve for a third straight day. Per CME FedWatch, September hike odds moved to 57% from 34%, a 2026 hike to 89% from 75%, two hikes are 51% priced this year and 100% priced by March, ahead of the September 15-16 FOMC. DXY used the speech to break its 200-DMA and run to the 100-DMA, recovering everything lost since last week's Treasury buyback announcement.
3. The AI trade bifurcated — SOX -3.5% while the megacap platforms were bought The PHLX Semiconductor Index fell 3.5%. NVDA -4.61% to $217.48 handed back most of Thursday's +8.5% post-earnings surge and MRVL -10.28% to $216.62 extended rather than reversed its after-hours loss. On the other side, AMZN +3.97% to $266.43 after Evercore ISI raised its target to $355 from $315, MSFT +1.68% to $513.53, AAPL +1.63% to $319.70, GOOG +1.53% to $342.88 — lifting consumer discretionary +1.7% and communication services +1.6% to the top of the board. Technology -1.3%, utilities -1.1% and industrials -1.0% led six of eleven sectors lower; financials +0.3% was the only other gainer. The companies that spend the AI capex were bought on the same speech that sold the silicon supplying it.
4. The payroll benchmark revision came in far smaller than feared, removing the last dovish argument At the same 10:00 AM hour, the BLS published its preliminary benchmark revision to the establishment survey: payrolls in the year through March were marked down just 79,000, about 0.1%, against a Bloomberg survey median of -183,000. Retail trade carried the largest single reduction at -154,600. The final revision arrives with the January 2027 Employment Situation in February 2027. Stacked on Thursday's initial claims at 203K vs 208K expected and continuing claims down to 1.778M, and Wednesday's headline PCE at 3.7% y/y vs 3.6% with core in line at 3.3%, this describes a labor market that is slow rather than cracking. Chicago Fed's Austan Goolsbee, from the symposium, said a couple of benign readings "doesn't feel like out of the woods." That is why a speech with no commitment in it could move the 2-year 12 basis points.
5. Thursday night's after-hours verdict was overturned in daylight — and PYPL was the day's worst name The post-close tape had sold six of seven beats Thursday evening. Friday's cash session reversed most of it. WDAY closed +5.76% at $204.72 after trading roughly -7% after hours; AFRM +5%, GAP +12.9% on the Old Navy leadership change, ESTC +17.4% on a full-year guide above consensus, with CRM +3% and NOW +3% extending. Only MRVL -10.28%, RBRK -11% and ADSK -4% held their losses. The worst-performing S&P 500 component of the day was not a tech name: PYPL -12.71% to $53.66 after Bloomberg reported the Advent/Stripe consortium abandoned a leveraged buyout valued at more than $50B. Elsewhere, bitcoin gave back most of the week's gains and was rejected at its 50-week average, taking MSTR -7% and COIN -6% with it; gold fell back to prior-Wednesday levels; WTI was little changed; copper printed another weekly all-time high.

Tomorrow's Setup — Three Paths Into the Open

Levels that matter: today's close at $766.08 is the pivot. Above $771 = continuation regime; below $761 = reversal regime; chop in between = pin mode.

Bull path
Month-end flows defend the 20-DMA, the rotation keeps working, and SPY reclaims 772 into a soft ISM

The bull path does not require the hike to be priced back out — it requires the rotation to keep absorbing it. The mechanism is intact: AMZN +3.97%, MSFT +1.68%, AAPL +1.63% and GOOG +1.53% netted a 3.5% SOX drawdown down to -0.25% at the index level, and the productivity half of Warsh's AI framing is a genuine earnings tailwind for exactly those four. SPX held its rising 20-DMA and sits roughly 1% below its all-time closing high with the RSI still constructive; the equal-weighted S&P held its 20-DMA too. Monday's month-end rebalance, in a month where the index gained while the average stock lagged, mechanically produces a bid for the laggards into the last hour — which is the same trade as buying utilities -1.1%, industrials -1.0% and the Russell. From there the bull case needs one data point to go its way: a soft ISM Manufacturing Tuesday would take September odds back under 50%, pull the 2-year off 4.38%, and let SPY work 772, then 776 and 780. The fundamental floor survived the week intact — NVDA's $108B Q3 guide with China at zero, MRVL's raised FY27/FY28 targets of ~$12B and ~$18B, ESTC +17.4%, WDAY +5.76% and CRM +3% all say demand is not the problem. Expression: buy the funding sectors rather than the leaders, keep the front-week convexity on regardless, and treat a Monday close above 772 with the equal-weight tape green as confirmation the rotation has another leg.

Base case
Chop between 766 and 772 into month-end, with the real decision deferred to Friday's payroll print

Base case, and the most likely. SPY opens Monday near $769.35 on its 20-DMA with no fresh catalyst — there were no after-hours earnings Friday night and the weekend tape has been quiet — so the session is dominated by month-end rebalance flow rather than by any view. The index works 766-772 without resolving; the front end holds 4.38% because nothing before Tuesday can move it; and the internals continue Friday's pattern, with semis heavy, megacap platforms bid, and small caps lagging under the 50-DMA. Tuesday's ISM Manufacturing and Wednesday's Beige Book nudge September odds around the 57% line without settling them, and AVGO after the close Wednesday becomes the week's first genuine risk event — it either generalizes the MRVL margin verdict across the complex or breaks it, and the SOX trades that outcome Thursday. Everything then waits for Friday's August employment report. Cross-asset holds its new configuration: DXY above its 200-DMA, 10-year 4.65-4.80%, gold consolidating, bitcoin heavy below its 50-week average, copper firm at weekly all-time highs. VIX is the instrument to watch — at 14.4 it has almost no room to compress, and a Monday that bleeds it toward 14 ahead of that calendar is an add rather than a signal. Posture: small in direction, long the front week, respect 766 and 772 on a closing basis only, and let the calendar do the work.

Bear path
Equities finally mark the front end, 766 fails, and the rotation runs out of places to rotate to

The bear path needs no new hawkishness — it needs equities to finish pricing what the bond market already did. The 2-year is 73bp above effective fed funds, the furthest since November 2022, two hikes are 100% priced by March, and SPX closed down only 0.25%. That gap closes one of two ways, and the fast way is the bear case. Trigger one: a firm ISM Manufacturing Tuesday or a hot ISM Services Thursday pushes September odds through 70%, the 10-year takes out 4.80%, and 766 goes on a closing basis. Trigger two is structural and already in place — the index only held Friday because megacap absorbed a 3.5% SOX drawdown, and that requires the rotation to have a destination. Utilities -1.1%, industrials -1.0% and financials at +0.3% are not absorbing anything, and the Russell is already under its 50-DMA having lost 1.5% on the week. If AMZN, MSFT, AAPL or GOOG stalls, there is no second line. Trigger three is AVGO Wednesday after the close: a margin guide carrying the same memory-cost signature as MRVL, HPQ -11% and NVDA's 71-72% Q4 trough would generalize the input-cost de-rating across the largest weight in the semiconductor complex — and Warsh has now made that same input cost a policy variable, so it would hit multiple and discount rate together. Below 766 the map is 762, then 760 as the trapdoor, with the rising 20-DMA the only structure in between and VIX starting from 14.4 with VVIX at 86.6 — almost no hedge on the book to slow it. It would run straight into Friday's August employment report, the one release that can make it worse.
